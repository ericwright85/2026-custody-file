# Case Theory / Trial Visualization Notes

This file is intentionally provisional. It should evolve from the indexed evidence and should distinguish evidence-supported propositions from Eric's personal interpretation of motive.

## Core questions

- What has actually changed since 4/15/2021?
- Which current allegations existed before 4/15/2021?
- Is the current schedule functioning for the children?
- Which parent more consistently centers the children's relationships, preferences, stability, and activities?
- What are the strongest facts for Sana, and how should they be understood in context?
- What remedy, if any, actually addresses a proven new condition rather than rewarding longstanding parental conflict?

## Eric's overall position

Eric's position is not that Sana is a bad mother or that the children should lose their relationship with her. He believes the children love her, benefit from meaningful time with her, and should have broad access to both parents.

Eric's present position is that the existing record does not show that Sana genuinely regards the children as unsafe with him, that the equal-possession structure itself is failing, or that a genuinely new post-4/15/2021 condition requires reducing his relationship with the children. Instead, the record contains years of recurring allegations, repeated statements that she "deserves" more time or that Eric does not "deserve" 50/50, and multiple examples in which cooperation, activities, or litigation peace are tied to Eric agreeing to less custody. The evidentiary task is to show this through conduct and chronology rather than by claiming certainty about Sana's internal state.

The strongest defensible formulation is therefore not "Sana believes none of her allegations." It is:

> Sana's stated reasons for modification should be tested against her own longitudinal conduct. Many of the same safety, responsibility, school, medical, co-parenting, and 50/50 objections existed before the current order, while her repeated custody-outcome language and conditional-cooperation statements provide a competing explanation for the litigation. The question is whether there is a genuinely new, substantial child-centered condition now requiring the requested modification.

## Historical regulated / conflict-state contrast

Eric has long described a marital/counseling pattern in which Sana's assessments of him changed sharply during conflict: severe or hurtful statements in moments of anger, followed later by calmer statements that did not reflect the same global view of him. A targeted historical Gmail review now provides **partial contemporaneous corroboration** of that broader recollection.

### What is now corroborated

A March 2014 email thread written by Sana while analyzing one of their fights contains several unusually direct admissions:

- she described a small dispute escalating into a much larger conflict;
- she wrote that she `said something to hurt you` — specifically, `I don't wanna be with you` — and later clarified that the statement did not mean she actually wanted the relationship to end;
- in discussing calling Eric an `ass`, she essentially asked him to recognize that she did not truly believe at her core that this defined him;
- she admitted refusing to leave him alone `out of spite`;
- she acknowledged bringing young Rayan into the adult dispute and later wrote: `In my mind I just wanted you to stop that behavior that kills me inside and for that reason I used Rayan.`

Separate emails also show sharply positive assessments of Eric's parenting/care:

- February 2013: Sana listed reasons Eric would be a `great daddy`, including his `love and care towards Rayan` and his preparation for Rayan;
- October 2017: she wrote that she loved `how great you are with her and how much you care for her` regarding six-month-old Aleena;
- January 2018: she wrote that she loved how Eric took care of `me and the kids every day` and said his `willingness` and `loving nature` amazed her.

These communications are summarized in `reviews/historical-relationship-email-evidence.md`.

### What is **not** yet corroborated

Eric also recalls marriage counseling in which this broader pattern was discussed in terms of fear of abandonment / an `abandonment wound`, and recalls Sana saying more explicitly that she says severe things in anger to hurt those closest to her but does not hold those views when regulated. That counseling-specific recollection remains uncorroborated in the evidence reviewed so far.

It should not be presented as a diagnosis of Sana or as a proven clinical mechanism unless supported by admissible records/testimony.

### Why this may matter

The potentially useful proposition is now narrower and stronger than a pure memory claim:

> Sana's own historical communications show that some severe conflict statements were intentionally hurtful, were later distinguished from her stable/core view, and coexisted with very positive assessments of Eric as a father and caregiver.

That does **not** establish that every later custody allegation is false or knowingly fabricated. It may, however, be relevant to impeachment, context, credibility, or the weight assigned to sweeping conflict-state accusations — especially if Sana presents her negative assessment of Eric as historically consistent.

Counsel should determine relevance, admissibility, remoteness, and any privilege issues before using this cluster.

## Motive / pretext theory

Eric believes much of Sana's presentation is pretextual and rooted in longstanding anger, identity conflict, traditional/gender-role expectations about motherhood, and a need to characterize him negatively. Those are **Eric's interpretations**, not facts that should be asserted as psychological conclusions unless supported by admissible evidence or expert testimony.

The safer trial formulation is behavioral:

- repeated "I deserve more time" / "you do not deserve 50/50" language;
- attempts to condition peace, compromise, activities, or cooperation on increased custody;
- recurring allegations that substantially predate the 4/15/2021 material-change anchor;
- conduct inconsistent with an assertion that Eric is actually too unsafe or incapable to exercise substantial parenting time;
- historical communications showing conflict-state accusations can differ from Sana's calmer assessments;
- evidence that Eric repeatedly offers transportation, flexibility, additional mother-child contact, counseling, swaps, and continued participation in activities.

If counsel believes gender-role bias is legally or strategically relevant, identify the specific statements/conduct that support it and avoid turning it into an unsupported motive diagnosis.

## Children's peace / flexibility theory

Eric's affirmative concern is increasingly about the children's experience of rigid possession conflict rather than his own desire for more time.

The working child-centered formulation is:

> The children appear to want meaningful relationships with both parents, less adult conflict, and more practical freedom over ordinary family life. Aleena has described feeling "like a ball" being passed between households without a voice. Eric's stated goal is not to remove Sana from their lives but to reduce the ability of adult conflict to block reasonable flexibility, activities, communication, and contact with either parent.

Eric states that if he received greater legal authority, he would not use it to cut Sana out or materially reduce her ordinary time. His proposed parenting philosophy is broad child access to both parents, subject to school, safety, and normal structure.

## Remedy caution

The phrase "strip Sana of all powers and custody" is not the preferred presentation. It is broader and more punitive-sounding than Eric's underlying objective, which is flexibility and de-escalation.

The remedy question should instead be framed functionally:

- What specific legal authority is necessary to prevent a recurring veto over ordinary child-centered flexibility?
- Would exclusive right to designate residence, tie-breaking authority on selected issues, expanded decision rights, or another conservatorship structure accomplish the goal while preserving substantial mother-child contact?
- If no legally sufficient material change exists, should the present order remain unchanged despite Eric's belief that a different structure might offer more flexibility?

Eric's own interrogatory position is important: he has stated that although more authority might permit greater flexibility, the facts motivating that view are largely not new, so absent a qualifying material change the current agreement may remain the legally appropriate result.

## Current narrative hypothesis

The litigation may involve longstanding conflict and allegations being presented as new circumstances even where the same core disputes existed before the material-change date. The evidence review should test—not assume—whether Eric consistently supports flexibility and the children's relationship with Sana while Sana more often frames possession and parenting through entitlement, conflict, or restriction.

The case should not depend on proving Sana's private psychology. It should depend on the observable mismatch between: (1) the requested custody outcome and stated accusations, (2) the pre-existing history of those accusations, (3) objective evidence of child functioning and actual risk, and (4) each parent's demonstrated response to the children's relationships, activities, preferences, and need for peace.

## Bad-facts rule

Any fact that materially hurts Eric belongs in the index and should be surfaced prominently rather than buried. Where an adverse statement has a legitimate factual or procedural context, preserve both the adverse reading and the contextual defense rather than treating either as established by default.
