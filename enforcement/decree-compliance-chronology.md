# Decree compliance / enforcement chronology

> **Purpose:** distinguish four different things that tend to get blurred together in a high-conflict possession record:
>
> 1. an **actual documented failure to surrender / permit possession**;
> 2. an **announced or threatened deviation** that may never have been completed;
> 3. a **genuine interpretation dispute / decree ambiguity**;
> 4. an **accusation of violation** that is not itself proof of a violation.

This file is a factual issue map for counsel, not a legal contempt conclusion. Bruman should apply the exact decree language, notice requirements, specificity requirements, defenses, and enforcement standards before promoting any item as an enforceable violation.

# Pattern analysis — trial-safe version

The record should not be presented as `Sana misunderstands every decree provision` or `every disagreement is a violation.` That would be disproved by the record itself.

A more supportable pattern is:

### 1. There are recurring possession disputes involving the same structural questions

- Does holiday possession overlay or reset the regular rotation?
- When does Spring Break begin/end if school calendars are unusual?
- Which summer designation controls?
- Can a parent redesignate after notice/deadline?
- Can a parent refuse a selected week because of pre-existing plans?

### 2. Some disputes are genuine ambiguities and get resolved

The 2023 Spring Break discussion is a good example: Eric initially took one position, consulted counsel, revised it, and the parties documented a practical interpretation.

That should stay in the file because it distinguishes **good-faith interpretation problems** from later knowing/refusal-type conduct.

### 3. Sana has taken an unusually strict position when the text/deadline benefits her

The clearest comparator is April 2023: Eric was two days late designating July; Sana refused to honor it and emphasized strict compliance with April 1.

### 4. In later summer disputes, Sana attempts to change or override dates after initial designations / deadlines

The contrast is clearest in 2026, where she attempts to replace the initially stated August period and ultimately says Eric will `take what I give you` or litigate.

### 5. Litigation / police threats recur around possession interpretation

The record contains:

- Eric's repeated references to a Father's Day 2021 police threat;
- March 2024 `bring the cops too` language;
- repeated `file with your attorney` / `tell the judge` / enforcement language;
- 2025 claim that Sana already had an enforcement ready to file;
- 2026 exchange messages referencing police and court.

Not all of these are improper. Their significance is the repeated conversion of calendar / co-parenting problems into litigation-enforcement conflict.

### 6. Four possession events deserve counsel's focused enforcement review

The best present anchors are:

**2021 → 2022 → 2025 → 2026**

That is potentially a much stronger narrative than treating August 2026 as an isolated mistake:

> **The current enforcement dispute appears against a multi-year record of recurring possession interpretation conflicts, advance refusal language, and several occasions where the scheduled exchange time arrived and Eric did not receive the children as he understood the decree to require.**

Counsel should decide which of those events are legally enforceable violations, which are merely background pattern evidence, and which are too remote or already resolved to matter independently.

The goal is not to get Sana to agree with Eric's interpretation. The goal is to make her identify **the exact language, chronology, legal advice if discoverable, and factual basis for each refusal or accusation**.

# Working enforcement thesis

> **The 2026 summer dispute should not be viewed in isolation. The longitudinal record contains recurring disputes over possession language, repeated advance statements that Eric will not receive possession as he understands the order to require, and several documented occasions where the exchange time arrived without surrender. At the same time, the record also contains genuine ambiguities and accusations against Eric. The strongest presentation is therefore not `Sana always violates the decree`; it is that the court should examine a repeated compliance pattern using the exact order language, with the four strongest possession events as anchors and the surrounding threats/misinterpretations as context.**
