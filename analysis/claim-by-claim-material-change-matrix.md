# Claim-by-Claim Material-Change Matrix

Status: working factual analysis based primarily on the completed 992-page TalkingParents review. This is not a legal conclusion. Each row should ultimately be tested against the operative decree, current pleadings, external records, and Texas law.

## How to use this matrix

For each asserted reason to modify, ask four separate questions:
1. **Did the issue already exist before 4/15/2021?**
2. **What actually changed after 4/15/2021?**
3. **Is the post-cutoff change objectively child-impacting, durable, and substantial, or mostly a continuation/escalation of parental conflict?**
4. **What independent evidence proves or disproves the claimed change?**

The defense should not argue that no problems ever existed. The more defensible position is that many current complaint categories were already part of the family baseline before the material-change date, and therefore repetition of those same categories is not itself proof of a new material condition.

## 1. 50/50 possession / schedule allegedly unsuitable

### Pre-cutoff baseline
- By 12/31/2020–1/14/2021, Sana was already saying the 2-2-3 / 50-50 structure was rough on the children and that she regretted supporting it.
- 1/6/2021: Sana wrote that she deserved nothing other than primary custody.
- 2/11/2021: Sana wrote that Eric did not deserve 50/50 custody.
- The broader desired outcome—primary custody / reduced Eric possession—was already explicit in 2019 and 2020.

### Post-cutoff record
- Immediate continuation of possession/daycare disputes after 4/15/2021.
- 2021–2022 includes summer planning, swaps, successful agreements, and repeated efforts to reduce unnecessary transfers.
- 2023: Sana expressly seeks standard possession and says Eric never deserved 50%.
- 2024: Sana expressly says avoiding litigation requires ending 50/50 and says she deserves more time.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: strong continuity issue.** The objection to 50/50 clearly predates the cutoff. The strongest possible modification case would therefore need objective evidence that something about the children or actual operation of the schedule changed materially after 4/15/2021—not merely that Sana continued to oppose the arrangement.

### Evidence that could change the assessment
- objective attendance decline attributable to transitions;
- sustained academic decline tied to schedule structure;
- therapist/evaluator findings that the possession pattern itself causes harm;
- documented chronic sleep/transportation disruption unique to the current schedule;
- reliable child statements over time showing the structure itself—not parental conflict—is harmful.

### Defense presentation
Lead with direct pre-cutoff opposition to 50/50, then compare that baseline with later identical rhetoric. Follow with objective functioning under shared parenting and examples where the schedule operated normally when the parents communicated clearly.

## 2. Poor co-parenting / communication

### Pre-cutoff baseline
Extremely strong. By 2019–2021 the record already contains:
- repeated accusations of harassment, lying, manipulation, and poor communication by both sides;
- disputes over schedules, school, medical care, activities, childcare, possessions, and decision authority;
- litigation-oriented messaging;
- highly charged and insulting communication from Eric;
- Sana's repeated claim that Eric could not co-parent with her.

### Post-cutoff record
The same pattern continues. There are also affirmative cooperation attempts by Eric: swaps, transportation help, recurring lunch/family-dinner proposals, offers to facilitate Sana's one-on-one time, activity accommodations, and summer scheduling.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: very strong continuity issue.** High-conflict co-parenting was not new after 4/15/2021. A modification argument cannot persuasively rely on the existence of conflict alone without identifying a qualitatively different post-cutoff development or new child impact.

### Eric risk
This is one of Sana's strongest fact patterns because Eric's harsh tone is extensive and visible. The defense should not minimize it.

### Defense presentation
Concede poor communication where supported. Distinguish communication quality from parenting capacity and schedule functionality. Show that conflict existed before the cutoff and that Eric simultaneously made repeated concrete cooperation proposals after it.

## 3. Unilateral decision-making / failure to exercise joint rights

### Pre-cutoff baseline
Already present before 4/15/2021:
- school transportation disagreements;
- Club Rewind / childcare disputes;
- medical / provider disputes;
- activity and scheduling decisions;
- repeated disagreements over who could decide what.

### Post-cutoff record
Potentially significant adverse examples:
- KRK transportation change implemented before agreement;
- martial arts enrollment dispute;
- some activity/childcare decisions handled first and discussed afterward.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: continuity with some potentially stronger post-cutoff examples.** The category itself is old. The key question is whether post-cutoff conduct became materially more frequent, consequential, or contrary to the decree.

### Evidence needed
- exact decree language allocating educational, medical, extracurricular, and psychological decision rights;
- school/provider enrollment records showing who authorized changes;
- frequency comparison before and after 4/15/2021;
- whether disputed decisions caused actual harm or were later accepted.

### Defense presentation
Do not deny the clean adverse examples. Argue proportionality: isolated or recurring execution mistakes in a longstanding dispute are different from a new condition justifying a major reduction in possession.

School issues were already heavily litigated in communication before the cutoff:

- transportation and bus decisions;
- morning schedules and bedtime;
- claims that one parent was historically more involved;

The same record also contains positive objective functioning, including strong grades / honor-roll evidence.

### Post-cutoff record
- positive school reports and all-A evidence at points;
- transportation disputes;
- later attendance/tardiness/discipline issues appear in the record and may be genuinely important depending on timing and cause.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: unresolved; requires objective records.** The allegation category is old, but actual post-cutoff attendance, discipline, or academic deterioration could constitute genuinely new facts if substantial and attributable to Eric or the schedule.

### Highest-priority evidence
- yearly report cards;
- standardized testing;
- attendance/tardy records by semester;
- discipline referrals and suspension records;
- teacher/counselor communications;
- custody-day overlay showing which household had possession before each issue;
- accommodations / special education records if any.

### Defense presentation
Use records, not parental descriptions. This category should ultimately be mostly quantitative.

- appointment attendance history;
- missed-appointment logs;
- portal messages;

### Defense presentation
Build a chronology of objective care and compare it to each allegation. Avoid arguing from isolated successful appointments if records show meaningful omissions elsewhere.

## 6. Safety / supervision

### Pre-cutoff baseline
Safety allegations clearly existed before 4/15/2021:
- helmet/supervision disputes;
- marijuana allegations;
- driving/transportation concerns;
- childcare concerns;
- general claims that Eric was too laid back about safety.

### Post-cutoff record
Later safety claims recur, including e-bike/driving/substance-related themes and some possession/exchange incidents.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: category strongly continuous; individual post-cutoff incidents may still matter independently.** A genuinely serious new safety event cannot be dismissed merely because Sana made earlier safety allegations.

### Evidence needed
- police / CPS records;
- photographs/video;
- medical injury records;
- vehicle/e-bike documentation;
- third-party witnesses;
- exact dates and severity of each claimed event.

### Defense presentation
Separate allegations into: corroborated serious event, ordinary parenting disagreement, disproved allegation, and unresolved allegation. Do not group them rhetorically.

## 7. Substance use / gambling / lifestyle

### Pre-cutoff baseline
Exceptionally strong continuity:
- marijuana accusations appear throughout 2019–2021;
- gambling and financial accusations were explicit in 2019;
- Sana tied those themes to parenting and custody before the material-change date.

### Post-cutoff record
Substance-related accusations continue. TalkingParents often contains allegation/denial rather than independent proof.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: strong continuity unless there is new objective evidence of materially changed use or child exposure after 4/15/2021.**

### Evidence needed
- criminal records;
- drug tests if any;
- medical records if placed at issue;
- witnesses to child exposure;
- contemporaneous photos/video;
- evidence of impairment during possession;
- financial records only if gambling is actually asserted as a parenting/material-change ground.

### Defense presentation
The relevant question is not whether marijuana/gambling was ever alleged; it clearly was. The question is whether there is a new, child-impacting post-cutoff condition.

- Eric accused Sana of telling Rayan he had to choose sides, blaming him for adult issues, or saying negative things about Dad;

- disputes over showing adult texts/materials to Rayan;
- repeated adult-emotional-burden concerns;
- Eric also has adverse allegations and some acknowledged poor wording.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: strong continuity, but potentially important best-interest evidence regardless of material-change theory.** The category was already entrenched. The strongest later events need corroboration before being elevated.

### Evidence needed
- therapist/evaluator notes;
- direct admissible child statements where legally appropriate;
- audio/video;
- neutral adult witnesses;
- school counselor records;
- prior custody-evaluation findings.

### Defense presentation
Do not call conduct 'parental alienation' as a diagnosis without expert support. Present concrete statements/actions and child impact. Distinguish Eric's reports from independently established facts.

## 10. Child behavior / emotional development as children age

### Pre-cutoff baseline
Some behavioral and emotional concerns already existed, but age naturally changes the form and seriousness of these issues.

### Post-cutoff record
Later years include more significant behavioral, school-discipline, autonomy, technology, and parent-preference issues—especially as Rayan enters adolescence.

### Material-change assessment
**Current classification: potentially genuinely new and therefore high priority.** A child's maturation, new mental-health needs, serious discipline problems, or changed expressed preferences may be materially different even if parental conflict is old.

The key is causation and remedy: does the new condition show that Eric's possession should be reduced, or simply that the child needs updated parenting/therapy/school interventions within the existing structure?

### Evidence needed
- discipline chronology;
- counseling and evaluator records;
- teacher observations;
- parent-by-parent consistency of discipline;
- child's stated preferences if legally relevant;
- whether problems occur across both homes or cluster around one environment;
- interventions attempted and results.

### Defense presentation
Do not reflexively classify adolescent developments as continuity. This category needs fresh, objective analysis because it is one of the most plausible sources of an actual post-cutoff material change.

# Provisional ranking

## Strongest continuity defenses
1. longstanding opposition to 50/50 / primary-custody objective;
2. poor co-parenting / communication;
3. substance-use and lifestyle allegations as categories;
4. possession/exchange conflict;
5. school/medical/decision-making disputes as categories;
6. mutual allegations of disparagement / involving children in adult conflict.

## Issues most capable of becoming genuine post-cutoff material changes
1. objective school attendance/discipline deterioration;
2. adolescent behavioral or mental-health developments;
3. a serious corroborated safety event;
4. a sustained new pattern of violating joint rights or court orders;
5. a significant therapist/evaluator recommendation based on new conditions;
6. a mature child's legally relevant preference, depending on context and Texas law.

# Bottom-line working theory

The TalkingParents record is strongest as a **baseline-and-continuity tool**. It demonstrates that nearly every broad rhetorical category later used against Eric existed before 4/15/2021. The next analytical layer must identify the narrower subset of post-cutoff facts that are actually new, then determine whether those facts are objectively serious enough and sufficiently connected to the children to justify the requested remedy.

That means the litigation should be organized allegation-by-allegation, not message-by-message.
