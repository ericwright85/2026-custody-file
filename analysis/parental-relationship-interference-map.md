# Wright evidence map — parental relationship interference / alleged alienation

> **Purpose:** map the existing evidence to conduct categories Texas courts actually analyze. This file intentionally avoids declaring Sana a `parental alienator` as a diagnosis or ultimate legal conclusion. The question is what conduct is proved, how it changed over time, what effect it had on the children, and how it bears on material change / best interest / remedy.

## Working legal vocabulary

Prefer these phrases in counsel work product unless Bruman decides otherwise:

- **relationship interference**
- **conduct hampering the child's opportunity to favorably associate with the other parent**
- **exposing the child to hostility directed at the other parent**
- **involving the child in adult custody / possession disputes**
- **interference with communication or possession**
- **ability to encourage and accept a positive relationship with the other parent**

These map more directly to Texas statutes and Houston cases than the broad label `parental alienation`.

See: [`../law/parental-alienation-texas.md`](../law/parental-alienation-texas.md)

# Baseline problem

The repository does **not** presently support a clean theory that relationship-undermining conduct began only after **4/15/2021**. Significant custody hostility and negative-parent narratives existed before the operative order.

A second, potentially stronger model is:

> **Rayan's 2025–26 deterioration is the objectively newer condition; the relationship-interference evidence is relevant to competing causation explanations, best interest, and whether Sana's requested remedy is rationally connected to that deterioration.**

# Evidence adverse to Eric / alternative interpretations

A valid relationship-interference analysis must include these:

1. Eric has sent hostile / insulting / psychologically characterizing TalkingParents messages, especially historically.
2. Eric has sometimes told children interpretations of Sana's motives (`she's mad at me`, `taking it out on you`, `panicking`, etc.). These may be intended as reassurance but can still place the child inside an adult explanatory narrative.
3. Heidi specifically instructed Eric to reduce discussion of Mom and focus on being Dad.
4. Ordinary parental discipline can explain some device restrictions and conflict; it should not be relabeled alienation without context.
5. Children's spontaneous preference for one home, frustration with rules, adolescence, school/peer conflict, blended-family transitions, and litigation exposure are competing explanations for some relationship deterioration.
6. Sana may possess evidence of Eric disparagement/interference that has not yet been produced. Proposed discovery expressly seeks it.

**Rule:** do not resolve these ambiguities by loyalty to Eric. Obtain evidence.

# How this can matter legally

## Defensive modification theory

If Sana is the movant, relationship-interference evidence may help test:

`NEW CONDITION → WHO/WHAT CAUSED IT → DOES SANA'S REQUESTED REMEDY ADDRESS IT?`

Even if Eric does not prove a new alienation-based change, the evidence can undermine a proposed transfer of more authority to Sana if the record shows she has greater difficulty supporting the children's relationship with Eric.

## Affirmative modification theory

If Eric seeks greater rights/authority, counsel must identify the actual **post-4/15/21 material change** supporting that affirmative relief. Candidate theories to investigate rather than assume:

- materially worsened direct child involvement in adult conflict;
- materially worsened interference with children's relationship/contact with Eric;
- Rayan's objectively new deterioration together with credible evidence connecting it to family conflict;
- materially increased inability to co-parent / prior shared-decision mechanisms no longer working;
- litigation/evidence retaliation or rejection toward a child, if proved.

## Best interest / remedy

Regardless of which party proves material change, relevant questions include:

- Which parent is more likely to encourage a positive relationship with the other?
- Which parent can acknowledge the other's value to the children despite conflict?
- Which proposed decision structure reduces the children's exposure to adult conflict?
- Does changing possession actually address the proved cause, or merely reward one parent's longstanding custody preference?

# Falsification tests

The relationship-interference theory should weaken materially if evidence shows:

- Sana consistently encourages the children's relationship/contact with Eric during conflict;
- alleged communication restrictions were ordinary, proportionate discipline unrelated to Eric contact;
- post-mediation rejection reports are contradicted by contemporaneous conduct/messages;
- Rayan's deterioration clusters with Eric's household or conduct rather than broader conflict;
- neutral professionals attribute the principal child burden to Eric or shared possession;
- Sana's current requested relief is supported by professional/neutral evidence connecting Eric/shared possession to actual harm and explaining why her remedy would help.

The purpose of this file is to expose those possibilities, not suppress them.
