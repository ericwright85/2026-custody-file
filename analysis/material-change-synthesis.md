# Material-Change Synthesis — TalkingParents Record

Status: working litigation analysis based on the completed 992-page TalkingParents review. This is not a legal conclusion and should be tested against pleadings, decree language, external evidence, and Texas law.

## Core factual proposition

The strongest fact pattern presently visible is **continuity, not emergence**. The major categories that later appear in modification rhetoric were already present before the 4/15/2021 material-change anchor: opposition to 50/50, claims that Sana was the primary caregiver, safety/medical/school criticisms, joint-decision disputes, allegations of poor co-parenting and disparagement, substance-use accusations, possession conflicts, and claims that Eric was insufficiently involved.

That matters because a later modification case cannot be fairly understood by looking only at post-2021 conflict. Much of the post-cutoff record looks like continuation, repetition, or escalation of already-existing disputes rather than a wholly new family condition.

## Temporal bridge immediately after cutoff

The post-cutoff record does not show a clean break on 4/15/2021. Within days, the parties were continuing the same daycare/Thursday-possession dispute. Summer 2021 continued the same themes of exchange timing, schedule consistency, flexibility, and legal interpretation. This supports the inference that the post-cutoff conflict grew out of an existing pattern rather than beginning after the cutoff.

## Post-cutoff repetition / escalation

By 2022, Sana was again expressly raising broad parenting, safety, emotional, educational, social, technology, and custody-evaluation concerns. By mid-2023 she was again directly seeking standard possession and writing that Eric never deserved 50%. In March 2024 she explicitly tied avoiding litigation to Eric accepting that 50/50 would end. Similar language continued through 2024–2025.

This chronology supports a candidate narrative that the 2026 modification request may be the culmination of a longstanding custody objective rather than a response to a newly arisen condition. That narrative must still be tested against any genuinely new facts after 4/15/2021.

## Facts that support schedule functionality

The record includes multiple indicators that the structure itself can function when the parties cooperate:
- successful schedule swaps and summer-week planning;
- recurring extracurricular attendance and transportation arrangements;
- examples of both parents reaching agreements without litigation;
- strong school performance at points under shared parenting, including pre-cutoff all-A / honor-roll evidence and later positive school reports;
- ordinary medical coordination and follow-up care;
- examples where conflict subsides once a concrete logistical agreement is reached.

This supports separating two questions: **Is the possession schedule harmful?** versus **Do the parents have a high-conflict communication pattern?** The record contains substantial evidence of the second; it does not, by itself, establish the first.

The record also contains facts that could support modification or undermine Eric's credibility if not confronted directly:
- Eric sometimes implemented decisions before obtaining joint agreement, including school/transportation, childcare, martial arts, or activity-related decisions.
- Eric's messaging frequently used harsh, accusatory, psychological, or demeaning language. Even where his underlying factual point may be strong, the tone can independently support a poor-co-parenting narrative.

These should not be hidden. The stronger defense is to separate **bad communication / isolated poor judgment** from a legally material change requiring a custody reduction.

## Working material-change argument structure

1. **Establish the baseline** as of 4/15/2021 using direct pre-cutoff statements and disputes.
2. **Map each current allegation backward** to determine whether it already existed.
3. For genuinely new post-cutoff facts, ask whether they are substantial enough, durable enough, and child-impacting enough to matter.
4. Separate changes in the children naturally caused by age from changes caused by the possession arrangement.
5. Separate parental conflict from schedule failure.
6. Compare alleged harm with objective functioning: grades, attendance, health, activities, stability, relationships, and actual outcomes.
7. Preserve adverse Eric facts and argue proportionality/context rather than denial where the record supports them.

## Highest-priority external evidence now needed

- texts, emails, recordings, screenshots, or third-party witnesses that corroborate disputed TalkingParents reports;
- calendars / actual possession history when a schedule violation is alleged.

## Current synthesis

The completed TalkingParents review strongly supports a **continuity defense**: the same custody position, same claimed historical caregiver imbalance, same 50/50 objections, and many of the same safety/co-parenting/decision-making allegations existed before 4/15/2021. The record also contains meaningful adverse facts for Eric, especially tone and occasional unilateral conduct. The central analytical task now is not to prove Eric was flawless; it is to determine whether the evidence shows a genuinely new, substantial post-cutoff condition affecting the children enough to justify changing the existing arrangement.
