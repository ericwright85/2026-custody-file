# TalkingParents systematic review — pp. 410–419

Source: `talking parents record(3).pdf`, pp. 410–419.

## Key findings

### 1. Co-parenting lunch / family dinner proposals — favorable
- 1/9/22 and 1/10/22: Eric proposes one-on-one co-parenting lunch, offers to pay, suggests making it recurring every 1–2 months, and says the goal is moving the relationship in a healthier direction.
- 3/9/22: Eric again proposes a recurring co-parent lunch and a family dinner at Gringos because Rayan wanted both sides together. He expressly says both parents should avoid subtext around the children and focus on creating a loving environment.
- Sana says the idea could be good eventually but conditions it on resolving other co-parenting disagreements first.
- Significance: strong support for Eric attempting repair / cooperative structures, though his surrounding language about Sana is often combative and undercuts the presentation.

### 2. Adult-conflict burden on children — favorable theme, mixed execution
- 3/9/22 Eric says there is “no room” for parental games around the children and proposes showing the kids a loving environment.
- 3/21/22 Eric says honest mistakes, lateness, etc. are not reasons he would file; he says he would only file over purposeful custody violations or conduct damaging the children.
- Significance: supports orders-as-floor / flexibility-before-enforcement theme, though tone elsewhere is poor.

### 3. Spring Break 2022 possession dispute — mixed / significant
- Sana initially states Eric’s possession would begin Monday 3/21/22 at 6 PM.
- Eric quotes a separate decree provision saying his week-on possession resumes Sunday 3/20/22 at 6 PM and says he intends to enforce it.
- Sana ultimately brings the children to Walmart on 3/20; Eric then says he does not plan to file because he received the children.
- Significance: potentially useful example of Eric enforcing only after notice and then declining litigation once compliance occurred. However, his messages contain extensive insulting/psychological commentary and threats of filing that can be characterized as high-conflict.

### 4. 3/11/22 Aleena spring-break exchange — unresolved legal interpretation / adverse optics
- Eric initially offers flexibility because he says the documentation is unclear.
- Sana cites decree language and says pickup should occur at school/Kids R Kids because Aleena was not in school.
- Eric insists Walmart is the official location, says he confirmed with counsel, remains there, and Aleena is transferred around 4:30 PM.
- Sana characterizes this as a violation/delay.
- Significance: preserve as unresolved decree-interpretation dispute unless underlying order/counsel communication independently confirms Eric’s reading. Optics are adverse because the child transfer was delayed during the disagreement.

### 5. Serious Eric tone problem / psychological labeling — adverse
- pp. 410–419 include repeated assertions that Sana has mental illness, narcissism, needs psychiatric help, is a “victimizer,” etc.
- These are not independently established by the thread and create substantial high-conflict / derogatory-communication exposure.
- Do not rely on or adopt diagnoses.

### 6. Email/account-control dispute — unresolved factual allegation
- Eric alleges Sana took control of Rayan’s email account and locked him out, and says he created a new account.
- The thread does not independently establish the account-access facts.
- Note: the record contains a plaintext password. Do not reproduce or retain credentials in summaries/indexes.
