# TalkingParents systematic review — pp.270–279

Source: certified TalkingParents record, pp.270–279. Review based on parsed page text; rendered images were unavailable in Files for this block.

## Key findings

### 1) 8/1/2023 — explicit no-50/50 / standard-possession demand
Sana writes: **“I will be filing real soon... You need to understand you do not deserve 50% custody time and never did. Are you in agreement to go to mediation and take the standard possession order for child custody?”**

Significance: high-value continuity evidence. This is an explicit statement that her opposition to 50/50 was not limited to a later/new factual development. It also belongs in the separate “deserve” language index.

Source locator: TP p.272.

### 2) 7/15/2023 father-son emails — direct evidence relevant to contact/alienation theme, but two-sided
Rayan emails Eric: **“you were talking negative about mom so she said I can't text or call you.”** Eric responds that Rayan is allowed to call/text him anytime, tells him not to push/argue, to **“honor your mother's authority but also speak your heart,”** and says he loves him.

However, Eric also tells Rayan that Sana “doesn't want you to talk to me,” may “heal and change,” and frames Sana’s restriction as her decision/fault. That is potentially problematic adult-conflict content directed to the child even though the message also contains pro-mother/authority language.

Significance: useful evidence that Rayan himself reported contact restriction, but preserve the adverse optics of Eric discussing Sana’s emotional state/intentions directly with Rayan.

Source locator: TP pp.272–273.

### 3) 7/17/2023 — Sana seeks standard possession / more time
Sana says the children are “very disturbed” at Eric’s residence and asks Eric to **“agree to take standard possession schedule so I can have more time with the children.”**

Significance: custody-modification position tied to broad emotional-abuse/safety allegations. Not literal “deserve,” but adjacent to the entitlement/modification timeline.

Source locator: TP p.274.

### 4) 7/17/2023 — alleged restriction of Rayan’s access to Eric
Eric cites Rayan’s own email and says restricting his ability to call/text dad is improper; he contrasts this with his stated policy of allowing contact with mom whenever Rayan wants.

Significance: potentially strong alienation/contact evidence if corroborated with phone logs, device restrictions, or other communications. Current record includes Rayan’s contemporaneous email, which is stronger than Eric merely reporting what Rayan said.

Source locator: TP pp.276–277.

### 5) 8/1/2023 — Sana again calls for custody evaluation/modification
Sana says she is concerned for Rayan’s mental health during Eric’s possession and that a child-custody evaluation is necessary. On p.279 she says it is “imperative” to have a custody evaluation **“to modify the current possession and access to the children.”**

Significance: further continuity of an active modification agenda during 2023.

Source locator: TP pp.277, 279.

### 6) 1/23–1/31/2022 punishment dispute — longstanding co-parenting/discipline conflict
Eric asks Sana for specifics about a serious punishment so he can reinforce it and explain it to Rayan. Sana says their parenting styles differ and later accuses Eric of micromanaging and undermining her by re-evaluating consequences. Eric’s eventual reply “Toxic.. ignored” is poor communication.

Significance: useful pre-2023 continuity showing discipline disagreements and claims of undermining were longstanding; also an Eric-tone adverse fact.

Source locator: TP pp.277–279.

## Adverse / caution items for Eric
- Major tone problem throughout these pages: repeated character judgments, psychological labels, and “be a mother” language.
- The father-son emails are mixed evidence. They support that Rayan reported contact restriction, but Eric’s messages also bring adult interpretations of Sana’s behavior into direct communication with Rayan.
- Alcohol allegation on pp.270–271 is denied as fabricated, but this block does not independently establish what happened. Do not characterize it as disproved without outside evidence.
