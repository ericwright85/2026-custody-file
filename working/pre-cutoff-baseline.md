# Pre-4/15/2021 Material-Change Baseline

Purpose: collect issues demonstrably present before the material-change cutoff so counsel can compare later/current allegations against the historical baseline. This is factual indexing, not a legal conclusion.

## July-August 2020 — phone / parental controls / co-parenting / custody threat
TalkingParents pp. 111-126.

- Sana objected to 7-year-old Rayan using Eric’s old phone, citing age, unrestricted internet, games, lack of parental controls, lack of joint decision-making, and concern that Eric was not co-parenting.
- Eric said the device was for communication with both parents, that Sana could restrict use during her possession, and later added automated parental controls and Google-family access.
- The parties disputed whether Sana had been given adequate parent/manager access and whether she had agreed to the phone beforehand.
- **On 7/26/2020 Sana explicitly wrote:** “I will have to continue to fight for sole managing conservatorship since you cannot co-parent.”

### Why this matters
This single 2020 thread contains several categories that recur years later:
- device / social-media safety;
- parental controls;
- unilateral decision-making;
- claims Eric excludes Sana;
- claims Eric cannot co-parent;
- access/contact between Rayan and Eric;
- threatened custody litigation / sole managing conservatorship.

This is exceptionally strong continuity evidence: these categories were not newly created after 4/15/2021.

### Important adverse facts for Eric
- Initial phone setup appears to have lacked the automated controls Sana wanted; Eric later added them.
- Eric’s messages contain substantial insulting/psychological commentary.
- There is a genuine factual dispute over whether Sana agreed beforehand and what level of Google manager access she had.

## December 2020-January 2021 — 50/50 itself challenged
TalkingParents pp. 469-474 and related threads.

- Sana said the 2-2-3 / 50-50 possession arrangement was “very rough on the children” and said she regretted supporting/testifying to 50/50.
- Daycare, routines, consistency and unilateral-decision complaints were already being framed as best-interest/co-parenting concerns.

## February-March 2021 — holiday possession / flexibility / best-interest conflict
TalkingParents pp. 181-185 and 461-464.

- Same recurring arguments already exist: parental fairness, equal time, flexibility, withholding, unilateral interpretations, best-interest rhetoric, and threatened exchange conflict.
- Eric repeatedly states that absent a specific agreed trade, following the written schedule is the safest way to reduce conflict.

## Overall baseline hypothesis to test as review continues
By 4/15/2021, the record already contains the core architecture later used to argue modification: alleged inability to co-parent, alleged exclusion from decisions, child technology/safety, schedule/possession conflict, parenting-style differences, claims 50/50 harms children, and threatened custody litigation.

Later passes should map every current allegation to its earliest supported occurrence rather than assuming it is new.
