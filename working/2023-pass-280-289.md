# TalkingParents systematic review — pp. 280–289

Source: `talking parents record(3).pdf`, pages 280–289.

## Key findings

### 2023-04-05 through 2023-06-25 — activities / inclusion / cooperation
- Eric proposes Rayan soccer, explicitly asks Sana to participate for consistency, says he is happy to pay, and asks her to provide parameters if she prefers. TP p.280.
- Sana proposes multiple activities (K2, soccer, gymnastics, swimming) and follows up repeatedly about summer participation. TP pp.280–282.
- Eric says Rayan is not interested in K2, supports soccer/swimming/gymnastics, and offers to help watch the kids for Sana's work needs. TP p.281.
- Eric pays the full soccer amount and repeatedly works to get Sana added to the team/household communications, including entering her as second parent and forwarding confirmations. TP pp.283–288.
- Sana also actively coordinates activities, payments, uniforms, schedule details, and safety reminders. This is a balanced co-parenting section showing both parents involved.

### Child preference / child-centered decision-making
- On 6/23/23, Eric delays finalizing Aleena soccer registration because she seems unsure and says he is gauging her actual wishes before committing; he then completes registration shortly after. TP p.289.
- This supports the theme that Eric sometimes centers the child's expressed preference rather than treating activity participation as mandatory.

### Safety / adverse communication
- 6/21/23 Sana raises heat-advisory concerns, sunscreen, hats, and hydration during soccer. Eric replies only `#pretense and threats`. TP pp.288–289.
- Adverse communication fact for Eric: the underlying safety concern was concrete and warranted a substantive answer, even if the threat framing felt adversarial.

### Communication tone / mutual escalation
- 5/31/23 Eric tells Sana to be honest/forthcoming after discovering second-parent fields and contact info, but then characterizes her reaction as emotional struggle. TP pp.283–284.
- 6/6/23 Sana insists communications be through TalkingParents; Eric responds with `#pretense` / `#fightywords`. TP p.285.
- Preserve as mixed evidence: practical coordination is good, tone is often poor.

## Theme mapping
- Eric supports children's activities and offers transportation/payment/help.
- Eric includes Sana in activity communications and tries to get her portal/team access.
- Both parents show substantial extracurricular involvement.
- Child-centered vs parent-centered: Eric explicitly checks Aleena's actual preference before finalizing soccer.
- Bad fact: Eric dismisses a specific heat/sun safety concern with labels rather than answering substance.

## Evidentiary caution
- This block is not strong proof of one-sided co-parenting failure; it contains meaningful examples of both parents coordinating constructively.
- Do not overstate the portal/contact dispute as deliberate exclusion absent external registration records.
