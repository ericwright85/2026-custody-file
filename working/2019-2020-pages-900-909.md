# TalkingParents review — pp. 900–909

## High-value findings

### 1/23/2020 custody swap — functional cooperation
The parties successfully worked through a straight custody-weekend swap and confirmed the resulting possession dates. This is a useful example that practical schedule flexibility did occur pre-cutoff when the exchange was framed concretely.

### 2/7/2020 medical update / possession boundary
Sana asks whether both children attended school and notes that her possession begins at 8am if they are not in school. Eric responds with a same-morning update: Rayan felt well and went to school; Aleena went to a 9:40 doctor appointment, was doing well, and was then dropped at daycare. Eric says that if Sana wants strict 8am enforcement in similar situations she can call him and he will make the children available. This is favorable on medical communication and willingness to accommodate, though his tone about an “official complaint” is unnecessary.

### 1/27/2020 transportation / childcare dispute — major continuity theme
Sana proposes changing Rayan’s school address/bus arrangement to her residence and replacing Aleena’s daycare with a caregiver at her home. Eric objects that the current schedule should remain stable, says the children have equal weekday mornings, and offers to personally help on Sana’s mornings instead of changing childcare and transportation. He repeatedly frames his position as maintaining consistency and offers concrete help, including being at Sana’s house by 7am or earlier.

This dispute is important pre-cutoff continuity evidence for later recurring themes: transportation, school address, daycare/childcare, claims of “primary” status, joint decision-making, and whether one parent’s work schedule should drive changes to the children’s routine.

## Favorable to Eric

- Offers to pick the children up / get them ready so Sana can accommodate a longer commute.
- Emphasizes consistency with Rayan’s existing bus stop and Aleena’s daycare.
- Gives timely medical information about Aleena’s doctor appointment.

## Adverse / caution

- The message volume is excessive; even where the substantive point is reasonable, the tone can support a high-conflict/co-parenting criticism.
- Sana’s proposed childcare and transportation changes are not inherently unreasonable on this record; there is a genuine work-commute problem being discussed.
