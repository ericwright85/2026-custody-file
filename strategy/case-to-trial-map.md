This is deliberately a **workflow map**, not a substitute for counsel's legal judgment or courtroom procedure.

## 4. Evidence architecture

Every important proposition should use the same structure:

> **ASSERTION → PROOF → COMMENTARY / COUNTER-READING → LEGAL SIGNIFICANCE**

Proof should link directly to Gmail / Google Drive / TalkingParents / record source when possible.

[→ Clickable evidence registry](../index/evidence-links.md)

This lets counsel read a proposition in seconds and inspect the underlying source only if needed.

## 5. Depositions

Depositions should convert broad accusations into defined factual positions.

For every major claim, seek clarity on:

`WHAT HAPPENED? → WHEN? → WHO KNOWS? → WHAT PROVES IT? → WHAT DID IT DO TO THE CHILD? → WHY DOES THE REQUESTED MODIFICATION FIX IT?`

## 6. Witness map

Each witness should have a clearly defined purpose rather than simply `knowing the family`.

Working categories:

- Sana — her claims, chronology, factual basis, requested remedy;
- Eric — baseline, parenting conduct, child-centered flexibility, response/context;
- neutral counselors/providers — only for specific supported propositions;
- school personnel / records — objective attendance, discipline, interventions, outcomes;
- custodians / authenticating witnesses where counsel determines necessary.

For each witness eventually capture:

`PROPOSITION → EXHIBIT → FOUNDATION → EXPECTED TESTIMONY → RISKS / COUNTERPOINTS`

---

## 7. Exhibit map

Evidence should be grouped by what it proves, not just by where it came from.

Potential trial-proof buckets:

1. baseline before 4/15/2021;
2. longstanding custody objective / `deserve` chronology;
3. schedule functioning / flexibility;
4. neutral child-impact evidence;
5. genuinely new Rayan school behavior;
6. competing causation evidence;
7. activities / custody leverage;
8. Eric adverse facts and context.

Each proposed exhibit should eventually answer:

`WHY IS THIS NEEDED? → WHO INTRODUCES IT? → WHAT PROPOSITION DOES IT PROVE?`

---

## 8. Opening preparation

The opening should eventually be built from the case map, not written independently.

Working conceptual sequence:

1. identify the legal/factual question;
2. establish the pre-2021 baseline;
3. acknowledge the genuinely new fact rather than minimizing it;
4. identify the missing causation/remedy link;
5. explain the child-centered practical position;
6. identify the few neutral sources the court will hear from.

Counsel should replace this with his preferred courtroom structure and governing law.

---

## 9. Examination preparation

For each witness, maintain two separate documents:

- **direct-examination proof outline** — what facts/exhibits are needed from the witness;
- **opposing-witness issue outline** — what factual propositions need clarification or testing.

The repository should avoid scripting testimony. Its job is to make sure counsel knows **every issue, every source, every inconsistency, and every unanswered question**.

---

## 10. Closing preparation

The closing should ultimately be generated from a claim-by-claim decision matrix rather than from rhetoric.

For every requested modification:

1. What is the claimed changed condition?
2. Is it genuinely post-4/15/2021?
3. What objective effect did it have on the children?
4. What caused it?
5. What proof supports that causal conclusion?
6. Why does the requested modification address that cause?
7. What evidence or alternative explanation remains unresolved?

Then counsel applies the controlling law.

# The attorney / AI sprint model

Treat Bruman and Erika as **stakeholders and legal SMEs**.

A useful cycle:

1. **Repository delivers a narrow artifact** — e.g. deposition module, allegation matrix, exhibit cluster.
2. **Counsel reviews for judgment rather than document hunting.**
3. Counsel marks: `wrong law / missing issue / weak inference / inadmissible / more proof needed / strategically irrelevant`.
4. The repository is updated.
5. The next sprint attacks the highest-value uncertainty.

The goal is to conserve attorney hours for the things only counsel can efficiently supply: **legal standards, courtroom judgment, admissibility decisions, procedural strategy, and prioritization.**
