# Extracurricular pattern review — gymnastics and taekwondo

Status: targeted TalkingParents review focused on activity attendance, facilitation, and custody-linked leverage. This review distinguishes direct statements from Eric's interpretations and avoids treating every missed class as misconduct without corroboration.

## Core finding

The TalkingParents record contains a documented pattern of extracurricular participation becoming entangled with custody conflict. The strongest evidence is not merely that classes were missed; it is that Sana explicitly linked future extracurricular participation to having more possession time, and in October 2024 paired a threat to stop taking the children to activities with demands for more time, child support, and attorney's fees.

## Gymnastics

### Initial support / enrollment

- 6/12/2023: Sana initiated Aleena's K2 gymnastics enrollment, described it as an opportunity Aleena should have, paid initial dues, and asked Eric to set up the recurring payment. This is important context: she was initially supportive of the activity.

### Repeated attendance concern

- 7/11/2023: Eric wrote from gymnastics asking whether Sana was coming that day and whether the children would also attend soccer.
- 9/21/2023: Sana affirmatively said the children would skip soccer and gymnastics that day because they could use a break.
- 2/25/2024: Sana herself wrote that Aleena had missed "quite a few gymnastics classes maybe close to 8" and said they needed to be made up because Aleena had advanced to Level 2 and needed the practice.
- 2/29/2024 and 3/7/2024: Eric again wrote from gymnastics asking whether Sana was coming / whether the children would attend. On 3/7 he said he was waiting at K2 and asked for advance communication if events would be skipped.
- 10/8/2024: Eric wrote from K2 that Aleena was not there and asked whether Rayan would be going to soccer.
- 11/13/2024: Eric stated Aleena remained enrolled and enthusiastic about gymnastics, documented Sana's stated unwillingness to take her, and again offered to pick Aleena up, take her to class, and return her during Sana's possession time.

The record therefore supports repeated attendance/facilitation problems, but the exact number of misses attributable to each parent should be established with K2 attendance records rather than inferred solely from messages.

### Explicit custody-linked activity statement — 1/26/2025

On 1/24/2025 Eric wrote that Aleena had told him how much gymnastics meant to her and wanted to attend more consistently. He offered to handle transportation during Sana's possession without disrupting Sana's time.

On 1/26/2025 Sana replied:

> "Yes, I will make sure this is mentioned in the next modification case as I have more time with kids I'll be happy to continue taking them to extra curriculars as done previously. Thanks!"

This is a high-value direct statement. The most natural reading is that Sana tied willingness to continue taking the children to extracurriculars to a future arrangement in which she had more possession time. The wording should be presented exactly and allowed to speak for itself rather than overstated as a legal conclusion.

Eric then treated the response as agreement to resume gymnastics, reminded her of the Tuesday/Thursday class schedule, offered practical flexibility for late arrival, and on 1/28/2025 wrote from gymnastics offering to help get Aleena there. He repeated transportation/help offers on 2/5/2025 and encouraged Sana to attend Aleena's first competition on 4/25/2025.

## October 2024 activity / custody leverage cluster

On 10/4/2024 Sana wrote that she might stop taking the children to soccer or gymnastics during her possession time, asserting educational responsibilities and saying she had only 50% of the time even though she "DESERVE[D] MORE TIME WITH THE CHILDREN."

Eric responded that he could not force her to take them but repeatedly offered to handle the activities, homework, transportation, and logistics during either parent's possession.

Sana then wrote:

> "Unless you have an offer for me to have more time with the children, child support, and attorneys fees maybe we can compromise. I'm done with this discussion."

The next day she added that the "FIRST STEP" toward any compromise was for Eric to realize she deserved more time with the children and start that conversation.

This cluster is materially stronger than a generic 'missed activities' complaint because the activity discussion, refusal/threat to discontinue participation, and custody/financial demands appear in the same exchange.

## Taekwondo / martial arts

### Important adverse/context fact for Eric

The taekwondo history is mixed and should not be presented one-sidedly. In late 2021 Sana objected that Eric enrolled the children in martial arts without her agreement and with inadequate notice. The record contains her contemporaneous statement that Eric had made a significant decision without conferring. That is a real adverse fact and part of the joint-decision dispute.

### Participation / facilitation pattern after enrollment

Once the children were enrolled, the later record shows repeated Eric messages attempting to determine whether Sana would take them during her possession:

- 1/4/2022: Eric asked whether Sana took Aleena to the 5:00 class and whether she would take Rayan to the 6:30 class.
- 3/29/2022: Eric asked whether Sana was taking Rayan that day.
- 4/14/2022: Eric asked whether Sana was taking the children to taekwondo and requested that she inform him when she could not go.
- 6/19/2022: Sana said she had been trying to determine whether the children were still enrolled. Eric responded that they had remained fully enrolled and accused her of choosing not to attend. His accusation about motive is not independently established by the message itself.
- Later in 2022, Eric documented that he had unenrolled Rayan after Rayan lost interest, while Aleena remained enrolled and continued attending with Eric.

The safe conclusion is that taekwondo provides evidence of recurring participation and communication problems, but unlike the 2024-25 gymnastics record, the reviewed taekwondo messages do not yet contain an equally clean direct statement tying attendance to receiving more custody.

## Evidentiary significance

### Strongest supported proposition

A defensible formulation is:

> Extracurricular participation repeatedly became entangled with the parents' custody conflict. In gymnastics, Sana moved from initially arranging and supporting the activity to later threatening or declining participation, while explicitly linking continued extracurricular involvement and broader compromise to receiving more possession time. Eric repeatedly offered transportation and logistical help during either parent's possession.

### What not to overclaim

- Do not state that every missed class was Sana's fault without provider attendance records and custody-date overlay.
- Do not characterize the January 2025 statement as an explicit legal admission that she intended to harm Aleena; quote it and explain the custody-linked condition.
- Do not erase the late-2021 adverse fact that Eric implemented martial arts enrollment over Sana's objection.
- Distinguish legitimate decisions to skip an occasional class for illness, fatigue, schoolwork, or another activity from a broader pattern of non-facilitation.

## Recommended corroboration

1. K2 Academy attendance records for Aleena, 2023-2025.
2. Martial-arts attendance / belt-progress records, 2021-2022.
3. Custody calendar overlay for each missed or questioned class.
4. Payment records showing who maintained enrollment.
5. Any K2 emails documenting missed-class counts, makeups, level progression, or competition participation.
6. Any texts/messages from Aleena expressing desire to attend consistently, while preserving appropriate child-evidence limits.

## Presentation theme

This evidence is strongest as part of the broader **child-centered opportunity vs. custody leverage** theme, not simply as a complaint about missed practices. The contrast is between preserving a child's established activity across both homes versus making participation contingent on the adult custody dispute.
