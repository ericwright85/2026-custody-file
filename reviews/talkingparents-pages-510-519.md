# TalkingParents systematic review — pp.510–519

## Strongest findings
- **Positive medical co-parenting examples:** 11/23–11/24/19 both parents coordinate Aleena’s antibiotic; Eric thanks Sana for administering it, offers to keep Aleena home and watch her during Sana’s work time, and later during Rayan’s flu accepts pickup instructions and asks how he is doing. pp.510–512.
- **Longstanding health-framing conflict:** By early 2020, Sana repeatedly says children return from Eric sick / need strict routines and home remedies; Eric repeatedly says she is exaggerating or inventing illness. This health-care conflict clearly predates the 4/15/21 cutoff. pp.512–519.

## Adverse / caution
- Eric repeatedly uses severe language accusing Sana of “inventing illnesses,” manipulating the children, and potentially overmedicating them. This is strong tone/high-conflict evidence and should not be sanitized. pp.513–519.
- Sana alleges Eric came to her home uninvited during her possession to verify whether the children were sick; Eric admits going to the home but denies entering and says he stayed outside. The exact physical-access facts are disputed. pp.519–520 continuation.

## Theme impact
- Useful for **continuity/no-material-change**: medical/health disputes and competing accusations of neglect/exaggeration were entrenched well before the cutoff.
- Also contains clean examples of both parents cooperating medically, which prevents overgeneralizing the relationship as uniformly nonfunctional.
