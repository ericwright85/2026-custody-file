# TalkingParents systematic review — pp. 240–249

Source: `talking parents record(3).pdf`, pp. 240–249 (review ref `turn126file0`).

## Key findings

### 1. School participation / first-day flexibility (Aug–Sep 2023)
- Eric invited Sana to participate in the first day of school, pointed her to volunteer signup, and offered to adjust the KRK meeting time if she wanted to see the children earlier.
- Sana raised a legitimate concern about Rayan's bus timing; Eric adjusted from ~7:30 to aiming for 7:20 based on the posted time.
- Eric promptly shared Wednesday-folder materials and the completed "handle with care" form when asked.

**Themes:** school involvement; flexibility; practical information-sharing.

### 2. Minor execution issues: headphones / activity preparedness
- Sana repeatedly asked about headphones; Eric said he would handle them but had not yet placed them in Aleena's bag by Sept. 5. He then said he had them and would bring them to gymnastics.
- Sana also criticized Eric for Aleena lacking proper soccer uniform/ball at first practice.

**Assessment:** minor adverse logistics/organization facts, not major safety or custody evidence by themselves.

### 3. Soccer registration / access / cooperation
- Eric registered both children for fall soccer, provided team names and calendar info, and repeatedly requested Sana be added to team communication channels.
- When Sana's foot was swollen and she said she could not take the children to practice, Eric offered to take them and bring them right back afterward.
- Sana responded that Eric had refused reciprocal help during his time and characterized the relationship as parallel co-parenting.

**Themes:** Eric offers practical help; both sides dispute reciprocity.

### 4. Tournament and child-specific decision-making
- Eric RSVP'd both children for the Labor Day tournament and worked through cleats/payment logistics.
- After Aleena participated Saturday, Eric decided not to take her Sunday because he believed she was not ready, wanted rest, and did not want to go.

**Assessment:** supports child-specific judgment rather than reflexive activity maximization, though Sana framed it as failure to support the team.

### 5. Sana explicitly states modification is necessary (Sept. 1, 2023)
Sana says she is concerned for the children's mental and physical stability during Eric's possession and that **"it is necessary to modify the current arrangement."** This sits alongside contemporaneous disputes over food, sleep, soccer, and communication.

**Significance:** strong continuity evidence that the modification agenda and safety/capability allegations were already explicit in 2023, rather than emerging only recently.

### 6. Adverse Eric communication tone
Eric responds to Sana's food/safety allegations with mental-health language and says her conduct reflects a mental-health issue and is contributing to childhood trauma.

**Assessment:** poor communication optics; preserve as adverse. Do not adopt diagnosis or treat these characterizations as fact.

## Overall value
This block is more useful for **continuity** than for proving any single incident. Sana expressly says modification is necessary in Sept. 2023, while the surrounding disputes concern the same categories still raised later: school logistics, food, sleep, extracurriculars, communication, and parenting competence. Eric also shows several examples of practical participation/flexibility, but with recurring inflammatory language that weakens presentation.
