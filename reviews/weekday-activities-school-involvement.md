# Weekday activities, school involvement, and continuity-of-opportunity review

> **Purpose:** evaluate the factual record relevant to weekday possession / decision-making by asking a practical question: **which parent historically facilitates the children's recurring school, activity, volunteer, and developmental commitments across custody boundaries?**
>
> This is not a custody recommendation. It is an evidence map for counsel. Attendance, participation, and motives should be proved with provider/school records where available rather than inferred from isolated messages.

## Executive finding

The repository previously had a useful but narrow gymnastics/taekwondo review. A broader sweep shows a substantially richer history across **Cub Scouts, gymnastics, taekwondo, soccer, first-day/school volunteering, classroom events, and school support**.

The strongest supported pattern is **not** "Sana never supports activities." The record disproves that overstatement: Sana initiated Aleena's gymnastics enrollment, later pursued a 2026 K2 waitlist opening, communicated with Rayan's soccer program, and originally enrolled Rayan in Cub Scouts.

The more supportable question is **continuity and facilitation across possession periods**:

- Eric repeatedly became operationally involved in activities after learning of them, volunteered in leadership roles, maintained schedules, offered transportation during either parent's possession, and tried to preserve participation when events fell on Sana's time.
- The record contains repeated instances in which Sana declined, questioned, or failed to facilitate established activities during her possession, including direct statements tying future extracurricular participation or compromise to receiving more custody time.
- The school-volunteer record contains affirmative Eric involvement, including first-day volunteer outreach, Watch DOGS participation, and direct coordination with Aleena's third-grade teacher on class events.
- The evidence is not yet complete enough to quantify activity attendance by household. A custody-date / attendance overlay would materially strengthen or weaken this theory.

This matters to any proposal that would concentrate **weekday possession or weekday decision-making** because the practical remedy question is not merely who values activities in theory, but who historically gets children to recurring weekday commitments and whether either parent allows the other to help when an activity falls during that parent's possession.

# Remedy-nexus question for weekday possession

The factual use of this evidence is narrower and stronger than "Eric does more."

For any proposal to move substantial weekday possession or authority to one parent, ask:

1. Which recurring weekday obligations exist?
2. Which parent has historically ensured attendance?
3. When an activity falls during the other parent's possession, who offers transportation or logistical help?
4. Does the possessing parent accept help so the child can continue the activity?
5. Which missed events are objectively documented, and which household had possession?
6. Have activities ever been expressly connected to demands for custody concessions?
7. What school volunteering / teacher / coach contact demonstrates each parent's practical weekday involvement?
8. Would the proposed possession change predictably increase, decrease, or leave unchanged the children's access to established activities?

The current record provides meaningful evidence on questions 2–4 and 6. It is still too incomplete on questions 1 and 5 for a quantitative conclusion.

The case file contains a **longitudinal activities / school-involvement history beginning before the 2021 order**, with particularly strong post-order evidence around Scout continuity and 2024–25 gymnastics.

The best-supported proposition today is:

> **Eric has repeatedly attempted to preserve the children's established activities across both households by participating directly, volunteering, offering transportation, proposing swaps, and facilitating activities during Sana's possession; the record also contains multiple instances of Sana declining or inconsistently facilitating established activities and direct statements linking extracurricular participation or compromise to receiving greater custody.
