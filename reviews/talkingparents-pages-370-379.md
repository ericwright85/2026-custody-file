# TalkingParents review — pages 370–379

Source: `talking parents record(3).pdf`, pp. 370–379.

## Main findings

### 1. Taekwondo / activity consistency — mixed but useful context
- Eric repeatedly asks whether Sana is taking the children to taekwondo and asks for notice when she cannot. He later states Rayan was unenrolled because he lost interest, while Aleena remained enrolled, and ties her wavering interest to inconsistent attendance across households.
- Sana later says she is willing to keep the kids enrolled and thinks Rayan should join again.
- Useful for the theme that extracurricular continuity was a recurring co-parenting issue, but not one-sided enough to treat as a clean favorable event.

### 2. Harlem Wizards / event participation — mixed
- Sana says attendance depended on how the children were feeling and later asks Eric to respect her possession time if he attends.
- Eric thanks her for communicating and says he cannot attend because he is sick.
- This is not a strong event either way, though it adds to the recurring tension between child activities and possession boundaries.

### 3. Discipline thread — significant adverse communication optics for Eric
- Sana raises a concrete concern that Rayan is testing discipline boundaries and says she wants consistency between households.
- Eric’s response includes extensive accusatory psychological language, references to NPD, calling Sana a “scared narcissist pretending to be a parent,” and claims that she is projecting abandonment issues onto Rayan.
- This is a major bad-fact communications example. Even where Eric’s underlying concern was child discipline and emotional wellbeing, the language is highly inflammatory and can be used to support a high-conflict / inability-to-co-parent narrative.

### 4. Important favorable statement inside the discipline thread
- Eric explicitly says he teaches the children to love and respect Sana because she is still their mother, and that he will continue reinforcing that respect.
- This is useful to counter a pure alienation narrative, although it sits inside a broader thread with substantial hostile language, so context matters.

### 5. October 2022 email / custody intent — important continuity point
- Eric states: “I don't want to take away your custody, and I don't plan to (unless it gets to a point Rayan or Aleena need me to).”
- This is useful evidence that, even while raising serious concerns, Eric was not expressing a standing agenda to reduce Sana’s custody; his stated position was conditional on future child need.
- However, the surrounding email contains extensive unsupported psychological diagnosis language and references to abuse, which weakens presentation quality.

### 6. Child-to-parent communication dispute — unresolved factual conflict
- Eric says Rayan messaged him saying he was not allowed to call Eric and asks Sana to tell him he may call, adding that Eric allows calls to Sana.
- Sana directly denies that restriction and says call/text/Fortnite records would show the children communicate with Eric more during her possession than with her during Eric’s.
- Treat as disputed unless call/text records are obtained.

## Trial-use notes
- Strongest favorable item: the explicit statement that Eric did not want to take away Sana’s custody and only contemplated action if the children later needed protection.
- Strongest adverse item: the discipline / NPD communications are among the clearest examples of Eric undermining his own substantive position through inflammatory tone and amateur psychological diagnosis.
- Do not present the phone-contact allegation as proved without corroborating call/text records.
