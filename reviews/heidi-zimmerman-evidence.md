## Key chronology

### 2021-08-02 to 2021-08-10 — Sana asks Heidi for a primary-parent recommendation
Sana emailed Heidi after Eric filed an enforcement motion and asked Heidi to provide a professional recommendation about which parent was better fit to be the children's primary parent. Sana told Heidi that a prior evaluator had recommended Sana be primary and asserted that the 50/50 schedule was not working and was too much on the children.

Heidi declined. She wrote that she did not have enough direct insight into either parent's home life to formulate that type of letter, that time with both parents was vital to Rayan's well-being, and that she needed to remain neutral in Rayan's best interests.

Source: Google Drive, `heidi responding to sana asking for letter.pdf` (8/10/2021 email chain).

### Early September 2021 — Rayan asks to see Heidi
Eric arranged a session after Rayan asked to see Heidi. Heidi later explained that Rayan had previously felt he was in a good enough place to move to sessions as needed, with the understanding that he could ask either parent to schedule when something was bothering him.

This is important because the September session was not merely Eric independently deciding that Rayan needed treatment; Heidi's own account says Rayan asked to see her.

### 2021-09-08 — Heidi reports Rayan's statements and emotional presentation to both parents
Heidi emailed both parents after the session. She reported that Rayan came in with his own concerns, was tearful, and said: "I'm just miserable about how things are with my parents. I want to be able to be happy with my dad, my mom, and Kaleb."

Heidi further reported that Rayan felt caught up in how unhappy his mother was with his father, did not like hearing negative things about his father from her, loved both parents, and did not want either parent speaking badly about the other.

Heidi recommended more regular access and said Rayan asked to see her at least monthly. She expressly invited Sana to bring him in October.

Sources: `heidi to me -1.pdf`; `Heidi and Me - emergency session and Heidi on Sana.pdf`; `heidi to sana -1.pdf`.

### 2021-09-09 to 2021-09-10 — Sana says she no longer wants Heidi as counselor
Sana responded that she did not feel confident continuing counseling with Heidi and that Rayan would be introduced to another counselor. Sana alleged that Eric filled the children's heads with misinformation, said the same type of last-minute counseling need did not occur with her, and later accused Heidi of one-sided treatment and unequal communication.

Heidi disputed the one-sided-treatment accusation. She wrote that she had initially emailed both parents together, later communicated separately to reduce the volume of hostile back-and-forth, had tried to alternate who brought Rayan to sessions, and that the pre-maternity-leave tradeoffs had been equal. She said the later session imbalance was attributable to scheduling and Rayan saying he felt he was in a good place, not favoritism.

Most significantly, Heidi wrote directly to Sana: "I have always openly prioritized my role as Rayan's counselor and advocate. I am solely on his side. It was never supposed to be about either one of you. You, however, are choosing to make it about your needs right now and not his."

Source: `heidi to sana -1.pdf`.

### 2021-09-10 — Heidi privately explains why she expected Sana to leave
Heidi told Eric that she had expected Sana to move in a different direction when Heidi did not comply with Sana's request to write a letter recommending a custodial arrangement in Sana's favor. Heidi said she believed Sana hoped a different counselor might produce a different outcome.

Heidi also told Eric that Sana had made a decision to cut her out "to serve her own agenda" and that the counseling-provider decision should have been one the parents reached together.

Sources: `heidi to me -1.pdf`; `Heidi and Me - emergency session and Heidi on Sana.pdf`.

Important limit: these statements were expressly framed by Heidi as "off the record." They are highly informative for case investigation, but counsel should assess discoverability, privilege, admissibility, and strategic use before trial.

### 2021-09-15 — Heidi gives Eric corrective guidance too
This is important context against any simplistic claim that Heidi was aligned with Eric. After Eric proposed a family meeting and discussed whether Rayan should see Sana's statements, Heidi strongly advised Eric to let it go, avoid conversations about Sana unless Rayan initiated them, keep answers short, stop trying to get Sana to have an "Ah ha" moment, and stop reopening hostile email exchanges.

She said Rayan was taking on too much emotionally at age eight and that the parents' email exchanges did not paint either one in the best light.

At the same time, Heidi told Eric she believed he was well-intentioned and wrote that Rayan had "so much love and respect" for him, encouraging Eric to focus on being Rayan's rock.

Source: `heidi to me -2.pdf`.

This email is useful precisely because it is mixed. Heidi was not simply validating Eric. She criticized conduct she believed was worsening Rayan's burden while simultaneously affirming Eric's relationship with Rayan.

### 2021-09-28 to 2021-10-11 — provider relationship ends
Eric later emailed Heidi reporting additional things Rayan wanted to discuss with her and a list of concerns he said Rayan had raised after returning from Sana's week. Those underlying statements are Eric's report unless independently corroborated.

On October 11, Heidi told Eric that she wanted to continue seeing Rayan but Sana's negative disposition toward her and her work meant the parents could not collaborate effectively enough for treatment to continue. She said she was deeply saddened to part ways and offered a referral.

Later that day, Heidi clarified that the greatest obstacle was informed consent: because of the divorce, she believed she needed consent from both parents to continue treating Rayan and could face licensing consequences without it. She told Eric to be transparent with Rayan that his mother was no longer giving permission for Heidi to see him so he would not think the termination was his fault.

Source: `heidi to me -last.pdf`.

## Evidentiary significance

### 1. Strong third-party corroboration of Rayan feeling caught in the middle
This is materially stronger than Eric merely reporting what Rayan said. Heidi was the treating counselor and independently reported Rayan's words and emotional presentation to both parents contemporaneously.

The strongest point is not that Rayan rejected Sana. It is almost the opposite: he wanted permission to love and be happy with both parents and was distressed by being caught in their conflict.

### 2. The child-distress evidence points to parental conflict/disparagement, not necessarily to 50/50 itself
Heidi's September report does not say Rayan wanted less time with either parent. The child reportedly wanted to be happy with his dad, mom, and Kaleb and not hear negative things about either parent.

That distinction is important to material-change analysis: documented distress may support intervention around conflict and boundaries without proving that equal possession itself is the harmful condition.

### 3. Potential interference with provider continuity
The documents support the following sequence:
- Sana asked Heidi to recommend which parent should be primary.
- Heidi refused and emphasized neutrality.
- Rayan later asked to see Heidi.
- Heidi documented concerns that were unfavorable to Sana and asked for monthly continuation.
- Sana then stated she no longer wanted Rayan treated by Heidi.
- Heidi disputed Sana's accusations of bias and said Sana was making the counseling issue about her own needs.
- Heidi privately stated her belief that the provider change was connected to her refusal to support Sana's custody request.
- By October, Heidi said she could not continue without both parents' consent.

This does not require us to infer motive ourselves; Heidi contemporaneously expressed that interpretation. But Sana's competing explanation—concern about bias, communication, availability, and Eric's influence—must remain in the file.

### 4. Heidi is more credible as a mixed witness than as an Eric-aligned witness
Heidi criticized both sides. She rejected Sana's requested custody recommendation and later criticized Sana's decision to discontinue counseling. She also gave Eric direct corrective advice about involving Rayan in adult conflict, discussing Sana, and continuing hostile email exchanges.

That two-sidedness strengthens the usefulness of her observations and reduces the credibility of framing her simply as an Eric advocate.

### 5. Potential longitudinal link to later child-in-the-middle evidence
The September 2021 report documents, very early in the post-cutoff period, Rayan's distress about parental conflict and negative statements. Later TalkingParents entries include similar Eric reports about Rayan carrying parental emotions or being placed in the middle. The Heidi email gives at least one independently documented anchor for that broader theme.

## Eric-context / defense notes

The Heidi file also contains material that could be used adversely against Eric, but context matters:

- Eric considered showing or discussing Sana's statements with Rayan, but his own email explicitly wrestled with whether doing so would harm Rayan and said he did not want to reinforce a negative view of his mother. Heidi then advised him not to do it and to keep such discussions minimal.
- Eric's language about Sana in emails to adults was harsh and included speculative psychological framing. That is a communication vulnerability. It is distinct, however, from proof that he actually delivered those formulations to Rayan.
- When Eric disclosed Heidi's remarks to Rayan, Sana characterized that as "filling his head." A defense reading is that Eric believed a child who felt unheard benefited from knowing his counselor had validated his feelings. Whether that was wise should be separated from whether it was an attempt to alienate him from Sana.
- Heidi herself instructed Eric on October 11 to tell Rayan truthfully that Sana was no longer giving permission for treatment, specifically so Rayan would not blame himself. That shows that truthful explanation of adult decisions to a child was not categorically viewed by Heidi as improper; the question was how much detail, framing, and emotional burden was appropriate.

## Important limits

- Heidi expressly said she lacked enough direct insight into either home to recommend which parent should be primary. Do not overstate her as endorsing Eric's custody position.
- The September statements about what Rayan experienced at Sana's home were reported by Rayan to Heidi; Heidi did not personally observe the home conduct.
- Some Heidi comments to Eric were expressly "off the record." Counsel should assess their admissibility and strategic use.
- The October 11 termination appears to involve both practical/ethical informed-consent concerns and relational breakdown with Sana. Do not oversimplify it into a single-cause narrative.
- Eric's September 28 email includes additional child statements that remain Eric-reported unless corroborated elsewhere.

## Current assessment

This is one of the strongest external-evidence clusters located so far. Its strongest use is not "Heidi thought Eric should have custody"—she explicitly refused to say that. Its strongest use is that a treating counselor contemporaneously documented Rayan feeling caught in parental conflict, wanting relationships with both parents, and objecting to negative statements about his father; she encouraged continued counseling; she rejected a request to recommend Sana as primary; Sana then discontinued that provider; and Heidi herself disputed the asserted bias rationale and ultimately stopped treatment because she believed she lacked the necessary parental consent.

The same file also contains credible corrective guidance to Eric. That should be preserved rather than hidden because it shows Heidi was functioning as a genuinely mixed, child-focused professional rather than simply validating one parent.
