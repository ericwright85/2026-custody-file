# TalkingParents pp.550–559 review

## Main findings

- Health conflict continues in July 2020. Sana characterizes Eric's request for old flu documentation as custody-evaluation strategy; Eric repeatedly disputes that the children were sick and asks for advance notice/doctor involvement when truly ill. This is continuity evidence for the longstanding health-framing conflict, but also shows Eric using accusatory language and questioning Sana's motives.
- Strong child-property theme: Eric repeatedly states that the children's possessions belong to them, not either parent, and should move freely between homes. On 6/15/20 he says clothes, toys, etc. belong to the children and the key is allowing them access and building responsibility. This supports child-centered vs parent-centered framing.
- Eric expressly allows Rayan's phone to travel to Sana's home and says Sana can control when/how he uses it there (7/16–7/18/20), useful against a claim that technology access was intended to bypass her authority.
- Adverse/optics: several messages use harsh language ('lies', 'harassment', 'dysfunctional') and dismiss Sana's clothing concerns. Sana also makes concrete allegations that children arrive in pajamas, old/broken shoes, and missing belongings; treat these as allegations unless corroborated.
- No new literal Sana parent-time 'deserve' use located in this block.

## Narrative significance

This block reinforces that disputes later framed as parenting deficiencies—health, clothing, belongings, and technology—were already entrenched well before the 4/15/21 material-change cutoff. It also provides a useful recurring Eric theme: children's property belongs to the children and should not be treated as a parent's possession or leverage.
