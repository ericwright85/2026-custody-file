## Entry 1 — backpack / mother anger
The first page describes Rayan being at KRK playing soccer when his mother came and he asked to get his backpack. He writes that she left without saying anything, which made him so mad he did not want to see her right then. He says she later came back and started yelling at him, saying he should have gotten his backpack and that he had been told to get it and did not. He describes being left disappointed.

### Evidentiary value
This is not a neutral witness account; it is a contemporaneous child-authored description of how he experienced a conflict with his mother. Its significance is the emotional pattern: anger/yelling, Rayan feeling blamed, and his own reported disappointment. It is stronger for showing his subjective experience than for proving every underlying fact exactly as written.

## Entry 2 — Aleena hit him / mother allegedly laughed and did not correct it
The second page reads, in substance, that Aleena "smacked me in the face," then Aleena and Mom started laughing, and Mom said words to the effect of, "I didn't say he didn't deserve that." Rayan then writes that Mom did not get onto Aleena at all.

### Evidentiary value
If authenticated as Rayan's journal, this is direct historical evidence of Rayan perceiving differential/punitive treatment inside the home. It is especially relevant to the later hypothesis that he internalized a recurring "bad kid" role rather than simply experiencing ordinary neutral discipline.

## Entry 3 — explicit `bad seed` / `good egg` contrast
The third page is the strongest. Rayan writes:
- "Aleena is siding with mom"
- Mom/Aleena are saying that he is "a bad seed"
- Aleena is described as "a smart cookie" and "a good egg"
- he writes that this makes him "feel bad/mad"
- near the bottom he writes that he feels like "she does not love me"

### Evidentiary value
This materially upgrades the prior `bad seed` item. It is no longer just a later allegation appearing in Eric's interrogatory response; the underlying source exists and appears to be a child-authored journal page. The clearest supported proposition is that, at some earlier point in childhood, Rayan himself recorded that he was being characterized as the "bad seed" while Aleena was contrasted positively, and that he experienced this as emotionally painful and connected it to feeling unloved.

This does **not** by itself prove who first used every phrase, how often it occurred, or that Sana's parenting caused later school misconduct. It does strongly support a longstanding child-experience pattern that should be considered before anyone attributes his later behavioral problems simply to shared possession or Eric's parenting.
