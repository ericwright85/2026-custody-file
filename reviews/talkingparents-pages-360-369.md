# TalkingParents review — pages 360–369

Source: `talking parents record(3).pdf`, pp. 360–369.

## High-value findings

### 1) 11/2022 custody-evaluation/modification continuity
Sana proposes another child custody evaluation on 11/1/2022 and says that if Eric will not agree mutually, it may need to be court ordered. This is strong pre-2023 continuity evidence that custody-change/evaluation issues were already active well before later modification efforts.

Source locators: p.368–369; TP lines 319–325, 338–365 in current review output.

### 2) Child-contact / alienation-related dispute
Eric repeatedly raises concerns that Sana's technology restrictions interfere with Rayan's ability to contact him. In the Nov. 27 event thread, Sana frames the issue as Eric intruding on her quality time; Eric frames it as trying to accommodate what Rayan wants. The record supports a recurring conflict over whether child preferences/activities should override strict possession boundaries, but not a clean factual finding of alienation from this block alone.

Source locators: pp.361–364.

### 3) Reciprocal swap ultimately agreed and performed
After conflict, Eric and Sana ultimately agree to a reciprocal 7:30 a.m. Nov. 13 / Nov. 27 swap, and the record reflects the Nov. 13 transfer occurring. This is useful evidence that the parties could reach concrete reciprocal possession agreements even amid high conflict.

Source locators: pp.364–365.

### 4) Medical follow-up coordination
For Rayan's urgent-care finger injury, Eric immediately says he is coming to urgent care, then provides the follow-up appointment time/location; Sana later attends the follow-up. This is a relatively clean example of both parents participating in medical care.

Source locators: pp.365–366.

## Significant adverse / mixed facts for Eric

### A) 'Divorcing a parent' discussion
Sana reports that Rayan said Eric told him a child can 'divorce a parent.' Eric does not deny discussing the concept; instead he explains that he wanted Rayan to know he had options if he felt victimized. This is a meaningful adverse fact because it can be framed as undermining or destabilizing the mother-child bond even though Eric simultaneously says he wants the children to love and respect their mother.

Source locators: pp.367–368.

### B) Harsh diagnostic / insulting language
Eric repeatedly uses 'narcissism,' 'symptoms,' and other diagnostic or insulting language in routine co-parenting disputes. This remains a recurring presentation/tone vulnerability and should not be adopted as fact in case theory.

Source locators: pp.361–369.

### C) Missed substance in Nov. 27 swap discussion
Sana actually offered a specific reciprocal trade on 10/31/22. Eric initially missed it and accused her of failing to offer a trade, then later acknowledged he missed it and accepted. Important to preserve because it weakens any claim that her offer never existed.

Source locators: pp.363–364.
