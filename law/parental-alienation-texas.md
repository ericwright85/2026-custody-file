# Texas law research — parental alienation / interference with the other parent-child relationship

> **Purpose:** research memo for counsel. This is not a diagnosis of either parent and not a substitute for Bruman's legal judgment. Texas appellate courts more often analyze the underlying conduct—disparagement, involving children in adult disputes, interference with possession/contact, or conduct that hampers favorable association with the other parent—than require the label `parental alienation`.

## Short answer

Texas law gives potentially substantial significance to a parent's course of conduct that impairs a child's relationship with the other parent. Houston appellate cases repeatedly state that **`poisoning of a child's mind`** may constitute a material and substantial change and that conduct **hampering a child's opportunity to favorably associate with the other parent** may support redesignating conservators.

For this case, the strongest legal model is probably **not** `alienation began after 4/15/2021`. The existing record contains substantial pre-order parental conflict. The more defensible questions are:

1. Did the **degree, form, or child-level effect** of the conduct materially change after 4/15/2021?
2. Did a longstanding adult-conflict pattern increasingly migrate into **direct child involvement, pressure, fear, rejection, communication interference, or damaged parent-child relationships**?
3. Is Rayan's objectively newer 2025–26 deterioration the material change, with parental interference/conflict relevant to **causation, best interest, and remedy**?
4. If Sana seeks more possession/authority, would granting more authority to a parent who is proved to impair the children's relationship with the other parent actually address the demonstrated change?

# Statutory framework

## Texas Family Code § 156.101 — modification threshold

A conservatorship/possession order may be modified if the modification is in the child's best interest and, as relevant here, circumstances of the child, a conservator, or another affected party have **materially and substantially changed** since the prior order / qualifying agreement.

Official statute: https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/FA/htm/FA.156.htm

**Working consequence:** the moving party must identify the relevant baseline and prove the intervening change; modification is not justified merely because one parent has long disliked the existing arrangement.

## Texas Family Code § 153.002 — best interest

Best interest is the primary consideration in conservatorship, possession, and access.

Current statute: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/family-code/title-5/subtitle-b/chapter-153/subchapter-a/section-153-002/

## Texas Family Code § 153.134 — ability to support the other parent-child relationship

When evaluating joint managing conservatorship, the statute expressly directs the court to consider, among other things:

- parents' ability to give first priority to the child's welfare and reach shared decisions; and
- **whether each parent can encourage and accept a positive relationship between the child and the other parent.**

Current statute: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/family-code/title-5/subtitle-b/chapter-153/subchapter-c/section-153-134/

This is one of the cleanest statutory hooks for evidence commonly described as alienating or relationship-undermining conduct.

## Texas Family Code § 153.009 — child's wishes

In a nonjury trial/hearing, on application, the court shall interview a child 12 or older concerning conservatorship / primary residence, and may interview concerning possession/access or other SAPCR issues. The interview does not eliminate the court's best-interest discretion.

Current statute: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/family-code/title-5/subtitle-b/chapter-153/subchapter-a/section-153-009/

**Case relevance:** Rayan's preferences or experiences can matter, but a preference is not itself dispositive and should not be elicited by either parent for litigation purposes.

# Core Texas / Houston authorities

## Arredondo v. Betancourt, 383 S.W.3d 730 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2012, no pet.)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2012/14-11-00742-cv.html

**Rule / language:** the Fourteenth Court states that material changes may include **poisoning of a child's mind** and that a managing conservator's course of conduct that hampers the child's opportunity to favorably associate with the other parent may suffice as grounds for redesignating managing conservators.

**Why useful here:** this is Houston appellate authority squarely connecting relationship-interference conduct to the §156.101 material-change analysis.

**Limit:** Arredondo involved substantially different facts, including relocation to Mexico and other circumstances. Use the legal proposition, not the factual outcome as a prediction.

## In re A.L.E., 279 S.W.3d 424 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2009, no pet.)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2009/85399.html

**245th-origin case.** Appeal from Harris County's 245th District Court.

**Rule:** material-change analysis is fact specific; changed circumstances can be established by direct or circumstantial evidence. The court emphasized the need to compare conditions tied to the earlier order with later circumstances.

**Why useful here:** particularly relevant to the question whether a longstanding problem later **significantly and negatively affects the child** in a new way. The underlying issue was substance abuse rather than alienation, but the analytical structure is important: a pre-existing problem is not automatically irrelevant if its later impact materially changes.

## In re H.P.J., No. 14-17-00715-CV, 2019 WL 1119612 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] Mar. 12, 2019, no pet.)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2019/14-17-00715-cv.html

**Rule:** material change is a threshold determination; the movant bears the burden. The court reiterates that not every changed condition justifies custody modification—relevant changes must reasonably bear on the child's best interest.

**Why useful here:** supports demanding an actual `change → child effect → requested remedy` chain rather than treating a list of old allegations as sufficient.

## In the Interest of A.V.T.R., No. 14-19-00986-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2021)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2021/14-19-00986-cv.html

**Rule:** the party seeking modification bears the burden to establish the baseline circumstances and what materially changed afterward. The policy behind the threshold is stability and preventing constant relitigation over children.

**Why useful here:** directly reinforces the repository's baseline-comparison architecture.

## Townsend v. Vasquez, 569 S.W.3d 796 (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2018, pet. denied)

Source: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/1968722.html

**Relevance:** Houston appellate case involving evidence characterized as attempts to alienate a child from the other parent. The opinion emphasizes the trial court's central role in credibility and weighing competing testimony.

**Why useful here:** shows that alienation evidence can matter significantly, but also that contested allegations are not mechanically resolved by labels; credibility and evidentiary quality remain central.

## In re L.L. / In the Interest of A.P.L. & T.D.L., No. 01-23-00725-CV / 01-23-00843-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] Aug. 21, 2025)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/first-court-of-appeals/2025/01-23-00843-cv.html

**Harris County, 247th District Court. Particularly important modern analogue.**

The trial court found, among other things, that:

- the parents had become unable to co-parent effectively;
- prior conflict-resolution mechanisms became ineffective;
- Mother exposed the children to hostility directed at Father;
- Mother attempted to minimize or thwart contact with Father; and
- the relationship had become highly conflictual in medical, mental-health, and educational decisions.

The appellate court identified evidence including involving children in possession/discipline/education disputes, questioning them about time with Father, making communication with Father difficult by confiscating phones, interfering with Father's time, and disparaging Father. The court affirmed giving Father exclusive rights over primary residence and major decision areas.

**Critical nuance:** the appellate opinion also emphasizes that both parents loved the children, evidence was conflicting, and the trial judge was entitled to weigh credibility. The case is valuable because the modification was not premised on proving abuse or neglect; relationship interference and dysfunctional co-parenting could be relevant to material change and best interest.

**Potential Wright analogue:** the factual categories map unusually well to some current evidence themes: child involvement in adult disputes, alleged communication restrictions, possession conflict, decision-making conflict, and differential ability to acknowledge/support the other parent's role.

## Epps v. Deboise, 537 S.W.3d 238 (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2017, no pet.)

Discussed and cited in the 2025 Lerner opinion above.

**Principle:** interference with the other parent's relationship and joint medical/psychological decision-making can support modification.

## Champenoy v. Champenoy, No. 01-12-00668-CV, 2013 WL 3327328 (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] June 27, 2013, no pet.)

Discussed and cited in Lerner.

**Principle:** a course of conduct by one parent hampering the child's opportunity to associate favorably with the other parent may suffice as grounds for redesignating managing conservators.

## Gunther v. Gunther, 478 S.W.2d 821 (Tex. Civ. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 1972, writ ref'd n.r.e.)

Cited by Arredondo for the favorable-association principle.

**Value:** historical Houston authority showing this is not a newly invented doctrine or dependent on modern `parental alienation` terminology.

## In re Marriage of Chandler, 914 S.W.2d 252 (Tex. App.—Amarillo 1996, no writ)

Also cited by Arredondo for the proposition that conduct hampering favorable association with the other parent may support redesignation.

# Cases expressly using / discussing `parental alienation`

## Koenig v. Koenig, No. 14-16-00319-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2017)

**Relevance:** Houston case in which expert testimony described parental alienation and the evidence included children becoming involved in adult issues, alignment with a parent's agenda, derogatory statements concerning the other parent, anxiety, and interference with the other parent-child relationship.

**Use:** demonstrates that Texas courts can receive expert testimony using the alienation framework and can consider such evidence in material-change / best-interest decisions.

**Caution:** expert testimony and the specific record drove the result; the label itself was not a shortcut around ordinary proof.

## In the Interest of M.O.S., No. 04-24-00767-CV (Tex. App.—San Antonio Feb. 4, 2026)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourth-court-of-appeals/2026/04-24-00767-cv.html

The trial court entered temporary findings of **credible evidence of a history of parental alienation** that endangered the child's emotional welfare and temporarily made Father sole managing conservator while restricting Mother's possession. The appellate opinion describes extensive evidence of alienation, refusal of visitation/turnover, and discredited abuse accusations.

**Use:** demonstrates the possible seriousness of proved alienation-related conduct.

**Caution:** highly extreme facts; not a reasonable direct factual comparator unless the Wright evidence reaches similar proof quality/severity.

# 245th District Court-specific authorities / signals

Trial-court decisions generally are not published as precedents. The best publicly searchable material is therefore appellate review of 245th orders and current court information. Older 245th cases involved different presiding judges and should **not** be treated as evidence of Judge Lancelin's personal tendencies.

## In re A.L.E. — 245th origin, 2009

See above. Important for the material-change framework and for the proposition that later materially harmful effects of a continuing problem can matter.

## Robert L. Smith v. Grace Karanja, No. 01-16-01004-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2018)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/first-court-of-appeals/2018/01-16-01004-cv.html

**245th-origin case.** The First Court reversed a modification because the required material-and-substantial-change showing was not legally sufficient for the relief ordered. It emphasized that a circumstance already contemplated in the original decree is not transformed into a changed circumstance merely because the anticipated event later occurs.

**Very important to Wright:** this is unusually close to the central defense concept: **an already-known or anticipated condition cannot simply be repackaged as the post-order material change.**

## Kahrim A. Bridges v. Carol Ann Pugh, No. 01-22-00027-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] May 11, 2023)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/first-court-of-appeals/2023/01-22-00027-cv.html

**245th-origin modification appeal.** The case concerned a default modification with very sparse testimony. Useful primarily as a procedural reminder that conservatorship changes still require an evidentiary foundation; it is not an alienation analogue.

## In re E.E., No. 14-16-00685-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] Sept. 26, 2017)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2017/14-16-00685-cv.html

**245th-origin modification case** involving restrictions/supervised possession. Useful for remedy/restriction research if Sana or Eric seeks severe possession restrictions; not presently an alienation analogue.

## Patterson v. Brist, No. 01-05-00798-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] Oct. 26, 2006)

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/first-court-of-appeals/2006/83585.html

**245th-origin conservatorship modification.** Includes child preference / best-interest issues. Historical only; different judicial era.

# Material-change problem in the Wright case

## Theory A — `alienation began after 2021`

**Current assessment:** likely factually weak. The repository contains significant pre-4/15/21 hostility, custody disagreement, and negative-parent narratives. Do not build a theory that depends on pretending those did not exist.

## Theory B — `same broad category, materially different degree / form / effect after 2021`

**Potentially viable factual question.** A pre-existing tendency does not necessarily mean every later manifestation was contemplated or unchanged. The relevant comparison could be:

`PRE-2021 ADULT HOSTILITY / DISPARAGEMENT`

→ `POST-2021 CHILD TRIANGULATION / CONTACT-PRESSURE / RELATIONSHIP INTERFERENCE`

→ `2025–26 MEASURABLE CHILD DETERIORATION / DIRECT REJECTION-CONFLICT`

This should be proven with dated evidence, not adjectives.

## Theory C — Rayan's deterioration is the material change; relationship interference informs causation and remedy

**Current strongest analytical model.** The school record objectively supports a newer behavioral/disciplinary condition. The unresolved questions are what caused it and what intervention is responsive.

Under this model, evidence that either parent undermines the children's relationship with the other parent becomes relevant to:

- competing causation hypotheses;
- best interest;
- §153.134's positive-relationship factor;
- whether awarding greater unilateral authority to a particular parent is likely to reduce or intensify child conflict; and
- whether a less disruptive remedy addresses the actual problem.

## Theory D — defensive use only

Because Sana is seeking modification, Eric does not necessarily need to prove an affirmative alienation-based material change merely to defeat her requested modification. The evidence can instead test whether:

1. Sana proved her claimed post-order change;
2. Sana proved Eric/shared possession caused the new condition;
3. Sana's requested remedy is connected to that condition; and
4. granting Sana greater authority is consistent with the statutory requirement to evaluate each parent's ability to support a positive relationship with the other parent.

If Eric affirmatively seeks new conservatorship rights, counsel should separately identify the exact §156.101 material-change basis supporting that affirmative relief.

# Proof categories courts appear to care about

Do not lead with `parental alienation syndrome`. Build proof around concrete behavior:

1. **Disparagement in the child's presence** — what was said, when, source quality.
2. **Child involvement in adult litigation / possession negotiations.**
3. **Questioning / recruiting the child for evidence or alignment.**
4. **Interference with communication** with the other parent.
5. **Interference with possession / activities / ordinary parent-child contact.**
6. **Conditional affection, rejection, guilt, or punishment tied to alignment** — if reliably proved.
7. **Professionals documenting the child being caught between parents.**
8. **Observable child effects** — anxiety, fear, behavioral change, school decline, conflict, secrecy, relationship deterioration.
9. **Which parent affirmatively protects the other relationship** despite conflict.
10. **Temporal comparison** — what existed at the baseline versus what is actually different now.

# Research standard

Case outcomes are not predictions. Trial courts receive fact-specific records and exercise substantial discretion; appellate opinions often defer to credibility findings when supported by substantive evidence. The repository should therefore use cases to identify **decision rules and proof patterns**, not to assert that matching a handful of facts guarantees a custody result.
