# Appellate decisions originating in the 245th

These are useful because they show issues arising from the same trial court, but most older decisions involved earlier judges and are **not evidence of Judge Lancelin's personal decision-making**.

## In re A.L.E., 279 S.W.3d 424 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2009, no pet.)

https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2009/85399.html

Modification affirmed. Important analytical point: a problem that existed before the prior order may still support later modification if the record shows its **later effects materially and negatively changed the child's circumstances**. The case concerned substance abuse, not alienation, but the before/after methodology is useful.

## Smith v. Karanja, No. 01-16-01004-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2018)

https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/first-court-of-appeals/2018/01-16-01004-cv.html

Modification reversed. Particularly useful proposition: if a circumstance was already contemplated when the original agreement/order was entered, the later occurrence of that anticipated circumstance does not automatically constitute a material and substantial change.

**Wright significance:** strong authority against repackaging longstanding, already-known conditions as the required new change.

## In re E.E., No. 14-16-00685-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2017)

https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2017/14-16-00685-cv.html

Modification case involving supervised/restricted possession. Useful if either side asks for severe possession restrictions; not presently a close alienation analogue.

## Patterson v. Brist, No. 01-05-00798-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2006)

https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/first-court-of-appeals/2006/83585.html

Conservatorship modification / best-interest case involving a twelve-year-old child's preference. Historical comparator only.

## Bridges v. Pugh, No. 01-22-00027-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2023)

https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/first-court-of-appeals/2023/01-22-00027-cv.html

Default modification appeal. Useful mainly as a procedural/evidentiary reminder that modification relief should rest on actual proof, not conclusory pleading.

## In the Interest of T.B.P., No. 14-24-00229-CV (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2025)

https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/fourteenth-court-of-appeals/2025/14-24-00229-cv.html

245th-origin conservatorship case tried to a jury. The appellate issues were jury admonishment / misconduct, not parental alienation. Relevant as a modern 245th case but not substantively predictive here.

# Practical conclusion for Wright v. Wright

There is **no responsible public-data basis** to say `the 245th likes alienation cases`, `Judge Lancelin usually favors X`, or anything similar.

The useful 245th-specific takeaways are narrower:

1. The same court has produced appellate decisions emphasizing the ordinary Texas **material-change threshold**.
2. `Smith v. Karanja` is particularly useful against reliance on circumstances already contemplated at the earlier order.
3. `In re A.L.E.` is useful in the opposite direction: a pre-existing problem can still matter if its **later impact on the child materially changes**.
4. Current public opinions involving Judge Lancelin mostly address procedural/mandamus issues and do not supply a reliable substantive alienation profile.
5. The safest preparation is therefore **law + auditable facts + explicit before/after comparison + child impact + remedy nexus**, not judge profiling.
