# Case Narrative Outline — v1

Status: presentation-level skeleton derived from the completed TalkingParents review. Needs external evidence and legal research before use.

## The case in one sentence

This appears less like a family situation that materially changed after 4/15/2021 and more like a longstanding high-conflict parenting relationship in which the same custody objections, same primary-caregiver theory, and many of the same safety/co-parenting allegations existed well before the cutoff and were repeated over time.

## Narrative arc

### 1. Start with the baseline, not the latest accusation
Before 4/15/2021, Sana was already saying:
- she should be primary;
- Eric did not deserve 50/50;
- the 50/50 / 2-2-3 arrangement was hard on the children;
- she had historically been the primary caregiver;
- Eric had problems involving safety, marijuana, gambling, school involvement, communication, and co-parenting.

The key framing question is therefore not “Are there conflicts after 2021?” There obviously are. The question is: **what materially changed after 4/15/2021 that was not already part of the family baseline?**

### 2. Show the continuity bridge
Immediately after the cutoff, the same kinds of disputes continued: daycare, Thursday possession, exchanges, summer scheduling, and legal interpretation. There is no obvious factual break where a new category of parenting problem suddenly appears.

### 3. Separate schedule from conflict
The parties are high conflict. That is not the same as proving the schedule is harmful.

The record contains examples of:
- successful swaps;
- agreed summer scheduling;
- cooperative medical communication;
- strong school performance;
- participation in activities;
- ordinary parenting across both homes.

This allows a clean distinction: **the parents' communication can be poor while the children's shared-parenting structure can still function.**

### 4. Show Eric's child-relationship conduct
The strongest affirmative story is not that Eric is conflict-free. It is that he repeatedly acts to preserve relationships and opportunities for the children:
- offers Sana one-on-one time with Rayan;
- proposes swaps so Rayan can attend activities;
- invites visible co-parent cooperation because it benefits the children;
- proposes fewer unnecessary transitions;
- supports school, medical, counseling, and extracurricular participation;
- repeatedly tells Rayan to love and respect his mother, at least in the written record;
- objects when children are placed in adult loyalty conflicts.

### 5. Contrast custody entitlement language carefully
Sana repeatedly uses language that she “deserves” primary custody or more time, including before the cutoff and later during modification disputes. This should not be presented as proof of bad parenting by itself. Its stronger use is longitudinal: it shows the requested custody outcome is longstanding and often framed as a parental entitlement rather than a newly emerging response to post-cutoff facts.

### 6. Confront Eric's bad facts before Sana does
A credible presentation should concede:
- Eric's messaging was often harsh and accusatory;
- he sometimes acted unilaterally or prematurely;
- there are disputed allegations of disparagement in front of the children;
- he has also, at times, mixed possession and litigation leverage;
- marijuana/gambling/safety allegations require objective evidence.

The argument should not be “Eric did nothing wrong.” It should be: **these facts either predate the cutoff, are isolated or correctable parenting/co-parenting mistakes, or do not establish that reducing the children's time with their father is necessary or in their best interest.**

### 7. End on the actual modification question
The final presentation should force each claimed basis for modification through the same sequence:

**What is the claimed change? When did it first occur? Was it already present before 4/15/2021? What objective evidence proves it? What actual effect did it have on the children? Why would the requested custody change solve that effect?**

If the other side cannot answer that chain with specific, corroborated evidence, the case risks becoming a relitigation of longstanding complaints rather than proof of a material and substantial change.
