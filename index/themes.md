# Main Themes

These are working themes to test against the evidence, not conclusions to force onto it.

1. **Alienation / undermining the dad-child bond** — determine whether this is a repeated, high-magnitude pattern rather than isolated mistakes.
2. **Child-centered vs parent-centered decision-making** — contrast decisions framed around what the children want/need versus what a parent believes they deserve.
3. **Eric supports the mother-child relationship** — willingness to facilitate contact, flexibility, and time with Sana even beyond minimum orders.
4. **Conflict is largely generated rather than inherent** — test whether ordinary parenting issues repeatedly become accusations, litigation, or conflict mainly through Sana's framing/escalation.
5. **The current schedule works for the children** — identify evidence of stability/functioning versus evidence that 50/50 itself is causing harm.
6. **Children seek flexibility, not rejection of mom** — requests for more time with Dad may be about autonomy/flexibility while preserving their relationship with Mom.
7. **Orders as floor vs orders as leverage/ceiling** — Eric often proposes flexibility around the children's needs; test whether Sana uses possession rights more rigidly or as leverage.
8. **Possession-time accounting vs child experience** — repeated focus on what each parent “gets” versus the child's activities/preferences.
9. **Same facts, different framing** — ordinary parenting differences sometimes reframed as “safety,” “co-parenting,” “best interest,” or violations.
10. **Longstanding pre-4/15/2021 patterns are not new material changes** — especially allegations about conflict, 50/50, co-parenting, safety, parenting style, and decision-making.
11. **Eric proposes flexibility before enforcement** — schedule changes, swaps, transportation help, activity accommodation, and default to orders only when no agreement exists.
12. **Child activities vs possession leverage** — test whether participation in camps/scouts/etc. becomes contingent on parent-time bargaining rather than the child's interest.
