## 4. Exclusive right to designate primary residence

**Request:** factual/legal basis for Eric's contention that Sana should not have the exclusive right to designate the children's primary residence.

**Response:** Eric answered that Sana struggles to separate anger/conflict with him from decisions involving the children and that unilateral residence authority creates a risk of restricting the children's relationship with him or uprooting them. He identified, among other examples:

- removal of Rayan from Keith Elementary during an earlier period in which Sana wanted to leave the area;
- conflict with Katy Maupin / Kiddie Academy;
- attempts to reduce or condition Eric's possession;
- the 2025 and 2026 summer-possession disputes;
- the April 4, 2026 statement that Eric could “take what I give you” or file with Bruman;
- cooperation/flexibility allegedly conditioned on Eric accepting less possession; and
- statements that the children do not want to be with Eric despite their expressed desire to maintain their relationship/time with him.

Eric further answered that he encourages the children's relationship with Sana, permits calls and additional contact/visits during his possession, and does not believe Sana would similarly protect their relationship with him if she held unilateral residence authority.

## 5. Exclusive educational authority

**Response:** Eric expressly stated that Sana's educational input can be valuable but opposed unilateral authority. He cited:

- attempts to reduce consequences imposed on Rayan at Willow Wood;
- the Keith Elementary move;
- Campus Kids / Lakewood disputes;
- communications with Aleena's preschool teacher concerning lunches;
- the Kiddie Academy / Katy Maupin conflict;
- accusatory or threatening communications with educational/childcare personnel; and
- a broader pattern Eric characterizes as unilateral decision-making followed by accusations that he refuses to co-parent.

Eric stated that he communicates with schools, attends activities, assists with schoolwork, and has proposed additional structure, academic support, environmental changes, and even temporary homeschooling if needed. His stated position was that educational authority should remain shared so adult disagreement cannot abruptly change the children's lives.

## 6. Exclusive invasive medical / dental / surgical authority

**Response:** Eric expressly acknowledged that Sana often shows appropriate concern for the children's physical safety and that her medical input is valuable. His stated concern is conflict/exclusion when anger, fear, or litigation concerns become involved. Examples identified were:

- conflict surrounding Rayan's broken finger;
- a Dr. Meadows telemedicine appointment connected to a psychological referral that Eric says prevented his meaningful participation;
- police/CPS/litigation escalation following Aleena's e-bike accident; and
- Sana's 7/15/26 call opposing an additional pediatric examination of Aleena.

Eric's answer concludes that both parents should remain involved and neither should have exclusive medical authority.

## 7. Exclusive psychiatric / psychological authority

**Response:** Eric opposed Sana having exclusive authority and stated that therapy should remain a neutral resource for the children rather than a custody/litigation tool. His answer relied principally on:

- the Heidi Zimmerman history: Dr. Meadows referrals, selection of Heidi, Rayan developing a therapeutic relationship, Sana later seeking Heidi's support concerning custody, and Eric's contention that treatment ended after Heidi did not support that purpose;
- Eric's reports that Sana calls the children bad/disrespectful, including the “bad seed” issue concerning Rayan;
- child reports/fear concerning anger, punishment, or withdrawal of affection; and
- Eric's history of supporting therapy, requesting family sessions, and inviting co-parenting counseling, including after Wilma Boone suggested it could help.

The served answer also contains stronger allegations concerning anger, emotional maturity, blaming Rayan for the divorce, and reported slapping. Those propositions require the same source-quality distinctions used elsewhere in this repository; appearing in Eric's discovery answer does not independently establish the underlying conduct.

## 9. Possession and access Eric contends each conservator should have

**Response:** Eric answered that the children are exhausted by the “her time, his time” narrative and by the idea that their parents are in conflict or dislike one another. He reported Aleena repeatedly describing herself as feeling “like a ball” because a ball is thrown back and forth and has no voice in where it goes.

Eric described telling the children that there is no “my time” or “her time”; it is their time, while acknowledging that the family must still follow the governing system/order. He stated that whenever he has the ability, he gives them flexibility to be with either parent or either side of the family. He also described reassuring them that adult anger is not theirs to carry while encouraging accountability and repair when anyone, including Eric, is wrong.

Eric concluded that although greater authority might theoretically allow him to provide that flexibility more than 50% of the time, **the underlying information is not new since the last agreement and therefore does not presently establish a material change supporting modification. His stated legally viable position is to leave the current agreement in place.**

## 10. Concerns about Sana's ability to act in the children's best interest

**Response:** Eric began: **“I believe Sana loves the children.”** His stated concern is that when the children's feelings, relationships, or choices conflict with what Sana wants, she can experience that as disrespect/rejection/disobedience or Eric's influence and respond with anger, criticism, threats, punishment, or withdrawal.

The factual bases identified include:

- child reports that they fear telling Sana how they feel because she may become angry, punish them, or stop loving them;
- statements that the children are bad/disrespectful/do not listen, particularly concerning Rayan and the “bad seed” issue;
- increasing conflict as Rayan develops independence;
- an Eric-reported historical concern attributed to Anne Causey about family patterns later affecting Rayan;
- alleged disparagement of Eric, Desirae, Tory, and the children;
- questioning children about Eric's home and later presenting their statements in an accusatory narrative;
- use of possession, flexibility, activities, or cooperation as leverage;
- possession withholding / unilateral schedule changes;
- changes in support for schools, providers, or activities when conflict develops;
- extracurricular continuity disputes; and
- escalation of ordinary disagreements into court/CPS/police/custody-evaluation threats.

Eric's answer concludes that his concern is not lack of love for the children but whether anger/control can override recognition of their separate needs, their relationship with Eric, and calm decision-making during conflict.

**Evidence caution:** several propositions in this answer are Eric's interpretations, child reports, or allegations rather than independently established facts. They must be tested against primary sources and neutral evidence before being promoted as factual conclusions.
